## The standard I propose

**Tier 1 — owner-checkable. Only this may close a visual/UI turn.**
1. A cloudflare tunnel link that **I loaded myself** and observed 200 + first paint (`link-load-succeeded-gate.py` already does the 200 half structurally — keep it, it is the model).
2. A **screen recording**, not a screenshot, at 390x844, of the specific interaction he asked about, run end to end: scroll to the bottom, tap it, close it. A screenshot cannot show "laggy" or "i cant scroll". A video can, and he watches it in four seconds.
3. A **side-by-side**: approved mockup left, built page right, same width, same crop. This is literally what he asked for at `owner_3.md:154`.
4. One sentence naming what I could not verify and why.

**Tier 2 — machine evidence** (self-tests, diffs, verifier PASS, typecheck). Required for me, kept in the log, **never quoted at him as a completion signal**.

**Tier 3 — prose claims.** Never proof. Allowed only carrying rule 15's own label, "I did not check this."

**And the gate-authoring rule that falls out:** a gate may only accept an artifact whose existence *it* verified — a file on disk with an mtime inside this turn, a `tool_use` block in the transcript, a sha that resolves under `git cat-file -e`, an HTTP status it observed. Never a phrase in my own text. Retrofit the four gates above; then run §2's test across the remaining 201 scripts in one batch, because applying it to a single gate today produced two bugs, and the four checks I sampled produced four more.

**One cost of my own proposal, honestly:** a recording per visual turn is 20-60s of wall time and a real token cost, and it will feel like friction on trivial one-line CSS changes. The mitigation is scope, not exemption — video only when the ask names motion, scrolling, or a gesture; still image + side-by-side otherwise. His most repeated complaint is the round trip, and a wrong artifact costs far more than a recording.
