Recommendation

B (Warm neutral). The middle path that solves the actual problem.

You said "neutral, not cool" — that rules out A (pure ink is cool-leaning despite being "no color"). C (true minimal) is fully neutral but reads pretentious for mass-market booking. B is the only one that's both genuinely neutral AND fits a Swiss booking marketplace for beauty/wellness.

  • The brand color becomes #2C2622 — a warm near-black. Logo, banner, CTAs, ink — all this color. Not pure black, has a touch of brown in it, reads "warm but ink."
  • Section bgs cycle through 3 off-whites: #F7F2E9 (hero/general), #EDE5D2 (sand for "your stuff"), white (#FFFFFF) for transactional/search/listings, #D9CDB4 (taupe for testimonials), #2C2622 (warm ink for trust/CTA).
  • No chromatic brand color at all. The logo dot can disappear (or become a #C8B998 stone color for a tiny detail). Hero "Schöner" is just ink-bold — no accent color.

First-principles why this works: "Neutral" doesn't mean "no warmth." It means "no chromatic tension." Pure ink (#000) on pure white (#FFF) is technically neutral but reads COLD because there's no humidity in it. Warm-ink on warm-off-white is NEUTRAL in chroma but has temperature. That temperature is what makes a booking-for-self-care site feel welcoming instead of clinical. Aesop figured this out 20 years ago for skincare; the same principle works for beauty/wellness booking.

Honest pushback: if you take this direction, the cobalt brand identity is fully gone. The "Solen blue" hero accent, the brand-blue logo dot, the cobalt CTA — all swap to warm-ink. This isn't a tweak, it's a re-skin. Worth confirming you want that scope before I start changing tailwind tokens. (And you can always come back to cobalt later — none of this is permanent.)
