Monochrome + single pop archetype

60% white/grey/black · 30% ink type · 10% one vibrant pop · scroll to compare amber / orange / pink / blue pops on the same monochrome skeleton

What this archetype is

The most disciplined application of 60-30-10. Substrate is pure monochrome (white, light grey, ink black). Type is ink. ONE vibrant color is the entire brand personality, used only on CTAs + the smallest accents. Nothing else competes for attention. Brand color screams because it's the only chromatic moment.
References: Linear (indigo pop) Vercel (white/black + brand) Aesop (black + green pop) Apple Music (red pop) Glossier early days (dusty rose pop) The Ordinary (minimal pop)

My honest read of the monochrome approach

What you gain:
  • The brand color punches HARDER than in any previous variant — nothing competes for attention
  • Photos + illustrations sit cleanly without fighting the chrome
  • Premium / modern register — Linear / Aesop tier
  • Easiest to maintain — fewer color decisions to make per new page
  • Solves the warm-vs-cool debate entirely (chrome is neutral)
What you risk:
  • Reads "tech startup" more than "beauty marketplace" — beauty audiences expect more warmth/color
  • Generic — Linear, Vercel, Notion, Stripe, Anthropic, OpenAI ALL use this. Solen would blend in
  • Loses the warm intimate feeling the fakejolart illustrations established
  • Dark anchor (BentoBusiness) loses its "we mean business" cool moment — ink is neutral, not branded

My pick if going this direction: D (hot pink) is the most beauty-coded — it pulls the brand away from "tech startup" risk because pink reads emotional/feminine/indulgence. C (orange) keeps your established equity. B (amber) is the safest evolution from current. E (blue) I'd skip — it leans hardest into the tech-startup risk for a beauty brand.
