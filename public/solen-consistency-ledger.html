Consistency ledger

Every inconsistency we found and fixed, paired with the rule it now follows. The left panel is the drift that existed; the right panel is the locked behaviour. This is the visual companion to the rules in CLAUDE.md and LOCKFILE.md.

Before · the drift After · the locked rule
01 · Emphasis

Selected = blue, never ink

Ink and blue were both used to mark a selected chip or slot, so "this is picked" and "this is the commit button" looked the same. Blue now means "your pick"; ink is reserved for the one commit action.

Before
09:0009:3010:00
After
09:0009:3010:00
Rule. Selected state = bg-s-accent (#276EF1). Ink fill is reserved for the single primary commit.
02 · Commit action

The one commit button is ink, not blue

The booking pay button shipped blue, uppercase and letter-spaced, competing with every blue accent on the page. The primary commit is ink, sentence case, calm.

Before
After
Rule. Primary commit = bg-s-ink, weight 600, 15px, sentence case. Blue never fills a button.
03 · Availability

Availability is plain text, not a green pill

A green "free in 15 min" pill read like a success alarm and was fabricated urgency. Availability is quiet information: a next-slot in plain ink.

Before
Free in 15 min
After
Next: 14:30
Rule. No green availability pill (owner-rejected). Next-slot = plain text-s-ink with a muted clock. No invented "free in N min".
04 · Loading

Loading mirrors the content shape (Skeleton)

A bare spinner told you nothing about what was coming. A skeleton in the shape of the content keeps layout stable and signals what loads.

Before
After
Rule. Content-load = Skeleton matched to the layout. Spinners only for in-place button presses.
05 · Ornament

No decorative dots or status pips

Bullet separators and little accent pips were visual noise pretending to be structure. Spacing separates; nothing decorative carries meaning.

Before
4.9  ·  320 reviews  ·  Open now
After
4.9   320 reviews   Open now
Rule. No separator dots, no decorative pips, no fabricated counts. Whitespace is the separator.
06 · Hairlines

Hairlines use the border token, not ink-at-opacity

Borders written as border-s-ink/8 render a cool grey-blue that drifts from the warm stone border everywhere else. One border token keeps every edge identical.

Before
card edge
rgba(10,10,10,.08)
After
card edge
#E7E5E4 · s-border
Rule. Every hairline = border-s-border (#E7E5E4). Exception: on-photo + hover-darken variants only.
07 · One component

One date picker, two layouts (the merge)

Booking ran a bespoke 14-day strip while search used the canonical month-calendar primitive. They are now one component: dateLayout="strip" for booking, "calendar" for search.

08 · Semantic colour

Universal-meaning elements keep their colour

A rating star was monochromed to ink during the B&W pass, which erased its universal meaning. Stars are yellow; this is not part of the 3% accent budget.

Before
4.9
After
4.9
Rule. Rating = s-star #FFC32B, available = green, error = red, price = ink. Universal colour is not the accent budget.
09 · Elevation

Shadows come from the elevation scale

Ad-hoc rgba drop shadows made cards sit at random heights. The elevation tokens give one soft, consistent lift.

Before
photo card
After
photo card
Rule. Use shadow-elevation-1/2/3. No hand-written box-shadow: rgba(...) in components.
10 · Tokens

No raw palette hex in components

Raw hex scattered the same colour across dozens of slightly-off values and made a palette change a manual hunt. Components reference the semantic token only.

Before
color: #16A34A;
After
class="text-s-success"
Rule. Components use semantic tokens; raw hex lives only in the token definitions. A palette change is then one edit. (Drift-checker rule A15.)
11 · Hierarchy

One ink anchor per card

When the name and the price were both bold ink, the eye had two anchors and no lead. The name leads by size; everything else steps down.

Before
Atelier Haarwerk CHF 89
After
Atelier Haarwerk from CHF 89
Rule. Name leads by size (the one ink anchor). Price and meta step down in weight and size.
