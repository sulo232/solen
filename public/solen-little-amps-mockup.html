Honest read

What works in B for Solen:

  • Cream substrate solves the warm-island problem. Your fakejolart illustrations (brick walls, golden tungsten) finally feel native — they're warm, the page is warm. No more "warm island in a cool brand."
  • Yellow CTA is louder than orange. Higher saturation + lighter value = more attention. Lift on click signal.
  • Coffee-brown anchor is unique. No other beauty marketplace looks like this (everyone defaults to navy/black). Real differentiation.
  • Dusty blue gives you the cool whisper you've been wanting (replaces section-arrow teal in a way that fits the warm palette).

What's risky:

  • Cream substrate is FAR from white. Every photo on the site (salon stock images, illustrations) will sit on cream — they need to be color-balanced for cream, not white. Some product photography will look dirty/yellowed.
  • Coffee-brown ink instead of pure ink is gentler but lower contrast — body text legibility on long pages takes a small hit.
  • Reads "indie coffee shop" more than "Swiss premium beauty marketplace." That's the brand bet — is Solen artisanal or polished?

My pick: Don't fully adopt B — but steal the GOLDEN YELLOW CTA and the DUSTY BLUE whisper. Keep white substrate (not cream), keep ink type (not coffee brown). You get the warm-bold energy without the artisanal-shop vibe.
