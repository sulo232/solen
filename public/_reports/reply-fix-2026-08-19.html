Why my messages confuse you

Every message I send starts with something from inside my workshop. A check, three boxes, the repair run. Your own question is never the first thing you read, so you have to hunt for your own thread.

Worked out by reading all 22 of today's exchanges, each one paired with exactly what you said back.

You asked “huh so what now is ths session finishd or”. My reply began “Session is finished on my side.” That is the only message all day whose first words answer what you actually asked. Your reply to it is the only real work order you gave me all session. When your question came first, you stopped hunting and started directing.

The four rules

1. The first sentence is about what you asked.Never about a check, a box, a batch, a run, or me.
2. The second line is what is different on Solen.A screen, a price, the actual words on a page. If nothing changed for your product, say so in those words.
3. My mistakes go at the bottom, under one label.Everything above it is something you can act on. Not deleted, moved.
4. The last line is your move, or there is no last line.Never my to-do list. All five times you asked why I stopped, that line was sitting right above your question.

What you stop hearing

  1. Machinery that broke in a way you never asked about loses the top of the message, and some of it will not survive to the bottom.
  2. My next task, every time. The risk is real: a message with no “next” line could read as me having stopped. Against that, all five of your stop complaints arrived directly underneath exactly that line.
  3. The detail of how a fix nearly went wrong, squeezed into one line.
  4. Nothing on length. Shortening was never the fix.

Nothing here changes Solen. It only changes how I write to you. Say the word and I switch it on.
