Three rules were corrected

These change what the rules SAY, not what the site looks like. No pixel moved. They are here so you can see them, because two of them admit the rulebook was wrong.

Rule text

A rule was citing companies that do the opposite

Our rule "never put blue on a big button" claimed Apple, Airbnb and Fresha as backing. I checked all three. Apple's own guidance says to limit brand colour TO interactive elements including buttons, which is the reverse. Airbnb's Reserve button is red. Only Fresha matches us. The rule stays, because you picked it deliberately, but it is now labelled a minority position rather than the industry norm it claimed to be.

Rule text

Two numbers we present as law were invented here

The "at most 30% bold" ceiling has no source. Awkwardly, this system had already thrown out a near-identical claim as folklore, then minted its own version of it the same morning. The rule still does real work so it stays, but it can no longer be cited as evidence. The "1.8x" rule turned out to be our own 28px heading divided by our 16px body, so it is arithmetic, not a discovery.

Rule text

Every semantic colour now has a legal and an illegal use

Our colour rule protected the hues but never said where each one may be used. That gap is the actual defect, and it is what the next section measures.

Other findings, also untouched

Not done

Body text at 14px is smaller than every major system's reading size

14 is right for dense rows, and Carbon and Atlassian agree. But for things people actually read, reviews and salon descriptions, Material defaults to 16, Apple floors at 17, and both Carbon and Atlassian step up to 16. We use one size for both jobs.

Not done

Our em-dash ban deletes correct German

There are 218 em-dashes and 30 en-dashes live in the four language files, so the rule is already widely broken. Worse, the spaced en-dash is a legitimate German construction. Banning both strips proper punctuation from our main market. Keep the em-dash ban, carve out the en-dash for German, French and Italian.

Not done

The text-expansion figure understates the risk exactly where it is worst

We plan for 15 to 35% growth when translating. The actual published data is length-dependent: short strings under 10 characters average 200 to 300%. Short strings are button labels, which is the one place we cannot absorb growth.

Not done

Icon-only buttons rest on a test nobody ran

Our rule allows dropping the label when an icon is "unambiguous". Only three icons are reliably understood without one: home, print, search. Copy, share and report are not among them.

Found while building the mockup

The homepage breaks our own type ceiling right now

Measured live at phone width: the home page renders 7 distinct font sizes against a ceiling of 4, and 4 weights against a ceiling of 2. The heading scale itself is fine, 32px at 2.67 times the body. It is the middle of the stack that has drifted.
