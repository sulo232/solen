Your refund process, answered

You asked six things. Every answer below came from reading your actual code and database today, not from memory.

1. Is there even a refund process?
YES, and more than I expected

A customer can file a complaint, the salon sees it, and you can step in above them. It is real, it is wired, and money genuinely moves.

2. How does it proceed?
  1. The customer filesPicks a reason: salon cancelled, did not happen, wrong amount, charged twice, unhappy with the result, other. Adds a description and photos.
  2. The salon sees it and answersIt lands in their dashboard. They can approve it or push back.
  3. You decide if it goes furtherYou can dismiss it, resolve with a note, refund it, approve, reject, or warn either side.
  4. The money actually movesIt comes back out of the salon's payout, and your commission goes back to the customer too. One place in the code does this, which is the right design.
3. Who decides?
The salon first, then you

Nothing is ever decided automatically. That is deliberate and it is written into the code as a rule. A person always makes the call.

4. Tax, and is it legal?
TAX IS HANDLED. LEGAL IS BEING RESEARCHED NOW

Swiss VAT already works: when a booking carried VAT and the salon is registered, a proper credit note is produced for the refunded amount. That surprised me. The legal questions, who actually owes the refund when you are the marketplace and the salon did the work, are with a researcher using real sources right now.

5. Build what is missing
RUNNING NOW

The gap is below. It is being built, and each piece gets attacked afterwards by someone who did not build it.

6. Do it as a loop
YES, running

The real problem: your deadlines are decoration

Every date those screens promise a customer is printed and enforced nowhere. I checked all 28 of your scheduled jobs and not one of them looks at a refund case.

what the screen sayswhat actually happens
Salon responds by (date)Never shown to anyone. Appears on no screen at all.

Solen decides within 3 business daysNo timer, no queue, no alert.

What this costs you: a salon that simply ignores a complaint blocks it forever, and the customer sits watching a date that means nothing.

What is being built

  1. A stuck case comes to you. If a salon does not answer in time, it escalates to you for a decision instead of sitting there. It never refunds on its own.
  2. The 14 day window becomes real. Enforced on the server, and shown to the customer while they still have time.
  3. Both dates appear where people need them. Same date for the customer and the salon, from one source, so they can never disagree.

Not touched: how much anyone gets back, or on what grounds. That is your call, not a build.
