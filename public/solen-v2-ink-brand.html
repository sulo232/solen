PATTERN: BRAND IDENTITY ≠ ACTION COLOR

Brand color where it shines. Ink where it acts.

Strategic pattern from Uber + Revolut + most mature consumer apps: brand color appears in the logo, hero italic accent, links, pulse dots, eyebrow text — but the action button is warm ink. Brand identity stays loud where you want personality; action buttons stay neutral where you want clarity.

Why this works (no inventing — observable pattern across mature consumer apps):

Uber rebranded to black-button-on-white in 2018. Their brand identity is the "U" mark + Uber Move typography — not a colored CTA. Revolut uses near-black action buttons; brand color appears in marketing surfaces + onboarding hero, almost never in transactional CTAs. Apple Pay / Apple Books / Apple Wallet all use ink-black action buttons. Aesop (closer to Solen's brand vibe — warm editorial beauty) uses a warm dark olive/black for action and reserves their amber accent for headlines + identity moments only.

The principle: brand color isn't a button color, it's an identity color. Putting brand on every CTA means the brand never gets a "this is special" moment because it's used so much it becomes background. Reserving brand for accents (italic words in headlines, pulse dots, links, "Alle →") makes those moments pop.
