Strongest depth, but vibe drifts. Cards pop hardest here, but the page starts to read as "earthy beauty brand" (Aesop / Glossier territory) rather than "modern booking app" (Airbnb / Linear / Fresha territory). This is the V2-D60 substrate that produced "beige collapse" when cards were ALSO cream — fixing cards to white removed the collapse, but the page still feels warmer than your "modern app" target.

What changed between A and B (the 0.5% nobody can name but everyone can see)

  • Page bg: #FFFFFF → #FAF8F2 (1.5% darker, 1.5% warmer). Below the threshold of "looks beige" but above the threshold of "white cards float."
  • Cards stay white (#FFFFFF) — V2-D60 fix preserved. The point isn't "make everything cream" — it's "give white cards a substrate to float on."
  • Same emerald CTA, same terracotta accents in headlines, same Peace Sans / Open Sauce One typography. Nothing else changes.
  • Banner pushback (separate decision): the blue (#1638C4) reads as a foreign element on all three substrates because Solen has no brand blue. Better fits: emerald (your s-brand — banner becomes a brand affirmation) or terracotta (your s-accent — fits a wellness vertical). Or drop the banner entirely until you have a real promotion.
  • App parity: a substrate like B translates cleanly to iOS/Android — warm enough to feel sensory on a 5" screen, cool enough to read as a booking utility, not a lifestyle magazine.
