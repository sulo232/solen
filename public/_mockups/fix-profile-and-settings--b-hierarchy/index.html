What actually changed. Two things, both just size and weight on text that is already on the page. First: on both screens, the small badge number, the grey subtext under each row, and the little header title at the very top all step to one matching secondary size, instead of splitting across three sizes that were 1-2px apart and impossible to tell apart on a phone. Second: on the settings screen only, your name grows from 20px to 28px, the exact size it already renders at on the profile page you tapped in from.
What this fixes. Your profile page's name was already a real 28px anchor at a 2.0x ratio over the 14px body, both already clearing the 28px / 1.8x floors. What it was missing was everywhere else: seven distinct sizes on screen, six of them crammed into one 8px band. After the fold: four sizes total (12, 14, 15.5, 28), and the tightest cluster now holds three. Settings had the harder problem, its own name never got the anchor treatment at all: 20px is 1.43x the 14px body, under the 1.8x floor. Matching it to the profile page's own 28px recipe gets it to 2.0x, the same ratio, for free, plus the same size-fold as the profile page.
What this does not fix. Neither screen gets a photo from this panel: both measure at roughly 1% photographic area against the 33% floor, and the fix for that is more real content higher up, not a type-scale change, a different variant of this same brief is built for exactly that. Weight share moves in the right direction as a side effect (folding the header title into a lighter weight) but is not fully solved: profile lands around 25.9% of visible text at weight 600+, comfortably under the 30% ceiling; settings lands around 30.4%, just over it. A third variant of this brief is built specifically for weight.
One number this brief handed me was backwards. I was told size-spread already passed on both screens and only the anchor needed work.
