Homepage colour review, three directions

"I don't like that it's just white." Three real answers, no hero photo, no dark theme.

Recommendation: Direction A, density. The floors law already names the mechanism for a page that reads too white: more real photography, never decoration. A doesn't touch a single token or invent anything, it composes the SAME SalonCard more times and one card-width wider, and it is the only one of the three that actually moves the white-pixel percentage of the screen, because it's the only lever that changes how much of the screen is photography.

Cost of B (surface rhythm), named rather than hidden: alternating grey bands down the whole page is a page-level pattern this system has never shipped at that scale. The closest precedent this system has, section background tints, was tried and reverted twice on this exact page (a peach tint was removed from the no-history rail; a background tint was removed from the nearby section, both per direct owner pushback on that page). B is a different mechanism (a shared sunken tray, not a coloured tint) but it is the same SHAPE of change the owner has rejected twice on this page before, so it carries real risk of a third rejection even though FLOORS LAW 4 supports it in principle.

Cost of C (semantic colour), named rather than hidden: honest and the cheapest to build, but thin. WalkInBand's green and the card star are the only two real semantic-colour elements this homepage has today, so promoting Walk-in higher helps but is unlikely, on its own, to read as an answer to "it's just white" the way more photography does.

A and C are not mutually exclusive; A already keeps every card's star yellow, and C's Walk-in promotion could ride along inside it. If only one ships, A is the one that answers the complaint literally.

  • A. Density More real cards, a wider first rail, one extra category rail: photography becomes a bigger share of every viewport.
  • B. Surface rhythm Same sections, same card count: alternating white and sunken bands (FLOORS LAW 4) so the page reads as bands, not one white sheet.
  • C. Semantic colour moments Same sections, same white background: the real green (Walk-in) moves to the second slot instead of the sixth, and every star renders slightly larger, the icon only, never the price or name.
