Why every mockup looked unfinished

The audit you asked for, in plain English. 7 design-law files were read end to end. The flaw is real, it is structural, and it is now fixed with your approval.

The verdict: the design law only had ceilings ("not more than 4 text sizes", "no dots", "less blue", "delete extra words") and zero floors ("at least this much"). Removing things never broke a rule; adding things could. So the screen that passed every rule most easily was the emptiest one. A compliant screen was a wireframe by construction. That is exactly the "unfinished" you kept seeing.

The 5 floors now in law

  1. Photos are required, not optional. Browse screens carry about a third of the viewport in real photography; a missing photo gets a designed fallback, never a bare grey box. (The B&W restraint only works when photos supply the life , that was always the theory; now it is enforced.)
  2. The full screen is the design target. Mockups show the IDEAL populated state from real seed data: full card info, 4+ items per screen, the next item peeking (so it feels alive, not empty).
  3. Every card edge must be visible. No white-on-white with an invisible 4% shadow; photo-less groups sit on the gray tray; gray and white alternate down the page.
  4. One clear anchor per screen. A 28px+ title or the photo leads; cards carry the name + price two-anchor rule.
  5. The finished-screen pass. Before anything reaches you: photo focal PASS, one biggest element PASS, a real number PASS, a color moment PASS, no dead-grey zone PASS. All five or it does not ship , a new gate blocks it.

What did NOT change

The restraint itself , the black/white palette, sparse blue, calm gray selected states, the type budget , is untouched. The audit confirmed those rules are right. The fix adds the missing "at least" half; it never loosens the "at most" half. Restraint stays; emptiness is no longer a legal outcome.
