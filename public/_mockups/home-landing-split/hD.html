hD . No persistent search, category-led. HOME at /de.

The bet: refuse the premise of the complaint. If nothing tries to sit between the logo and the hamburger, nothing is too small. The 250px three-field stack leaves the home screen entirely and the screen opens on what we actually have.

Navigation answer, stated so it is not implied

Search is a 44x44 icon button in a 64px sticky header, immediately left of the hamburger. It is a peer of the hamburger, not a field. Tapping it opens the real SearchBar collapsed layer full width, the panel above, which is Solen's existing SearchOverlay mechanism, so this is a geometry change and not a new system. The measured 243px gap between logo and burger stays deliberately empty. Category navigation lives in the 104px strip directly under the header, seven tiles with about 4.6 visible plus a cropped fifth, so the taxonomy is browsable without opening anything. City is resolved silently and then PROVEN in the section title "Popular in Basel", the Square Go move; changing it costs one tap on the search icon. The foot of the screen carries nothing at all. The sticky header is the only chrome on the page, which is exactly why the search glyph has to live in it: it is the one thing that never scrolls away.

The measured number that drove it

The first salon card on live /de sits at y=842 against an 844px fold. One pixel of salon inside the whole first screen. Removing the 84px header's 20px of slack, the 31.2px promise headline, the 18px subline and the 358x250 search stack is what buys it back. Secondary: WHY_ENTRY's four measured mobile entry controls are 55pt Airbnb, 51pt Square Go, 41pt Uber Eats and 0pt Fresha iOS. Every mobile entry control in that 24-app sample is one row or absent. Ours is 250. This direction takes the Fresha iOS end of that range.

Header 0 to 64. Category strip 64 to 168. Section title 168 to 224. First salon card top: y = 224. That is 618px earlier than today. Row 2 at 444, row 3 at 664 with its price line cropping at the fold, which is the scroll promise. Six salon cards inside the first viewport against a floor of four. Zero controls under 44px.

None of the numbers in this block are arithmetic. They were taken off the rendered page at 390x844 with getBoundingClientRect and getComputedStyle after the file was written, which is also how the 8px the browser adds by default got caught and zeroed. Eight images requested, eight loaded, zero broken, eight distinct sources.

Why there are eight cards and not twelve

I pixel-sampled the shared mockup photo pool before building. Twelve files, eight distinct photographs. p04 and p05 are byte-identical, md5 6dedd4f04585ecb69b7aacf50fe2bc7d, and p01/p10, p02/p08, p09/p11 each register a 64x64 mean-absolute-difference under 0.2, which is the same photograph re-encoded. Filling more slots would have put one interior under two business names on one screen, which is the second-ranked defect in WHY_IT_LOOKS_CHEAP and the most recognisable "marketplace with no supply" tell there is. So this renders eight cards, eight photographs, zero reuse. The asset pool is the constraint, not the layout. If other directions are being built from this same folder, they are all working with eight.

What this direction gives up

1. Discoverability of search, and the corpus puts a number on it. 29 of 34 home feeds put a search field or pill in the top zone. The 5 that demote it to an icon or a tab destination are Fresha, Resy, ClassPass, Skip and Nike, and every one is an iOS app whose user already installed it. Solen is pre-launch and is all-new-visitors by definition, WHY_ENTRY 4c. That is exactly the population that most needs the query language taught, and this is the direction that teaches it least. It is the sharpest cost in the set and I am not softening it.

2. The time constraint disappears from the home screen. A grid cannot express "Saturday morning". Someone whose binding constraint is when, not what, has to find the icon first. For an appointment marketplace that is the constraint deciding whether a result exists at all.

3. It loads the whole burden onto feed quality and ranking. Removing the search field removes the exit, so the first viewport now has to be good enough to browse. That is real backend ranking work, not a layout change.

4. It reads "put the search on the top" most loosely of any direction here. The only thing at the top is a 22px glyph in a 44px button. If he meant a visible bar, this is the direction that misses.

Six things surfaced rather than smoothed over

0. The card shows no category word, and that was a gate's call, not mine. I first wrote the meta line as the rating value, a small separator, then the category. The separator gate refused it and named the rule: when two bits do not differ in colour or weight, they go on separate lines, never on one line joined by a dot. Rather than spend a fourth text line I cut the category, because on a photo-first card the photograph already says barbershop or nail studio, and the category strip at the top of this very screen is the categorical navigation, so the word was repeating what two other things already say. The cost, named: a screen-reader user now hears the salon name and a price and no category at all, so if this direction is picked the category belongs in the link's accessible name even though it is off the visible card.

A. The brief's LOCKED list says card radius 16; the shipped SalonCard photo is rounded-[22px], CORPUS.md section 6, confirmed in SalonCard.tsx. I followed the brief's 16 literally. If the intent was to match the shipped card, this is a one-value change needing one sentence to settle.

B. The price sits in the meta line at body weight per the brief, but with a colour step to ink. The measured defect is that price, category and postcode all render at 12/400/#6B6B6B. A colour change is not a size or weight step, so it is not a second anchor, and every corpus app that shows a price buys the hierarchy back the same way. Say the word and it goes back to grey.

C. Two source paths in the brief do not exist as written. The search bar is at app/[locale]/_components/homepage/SearchBar.tsx, not _components/search/, which holds SearchOverlay, SearchTemplate and SalonResultCard. And HEIGHT.mobile.collapsed is 250 on disk today, while WHY_ENTRY.md line 420 still cites 280. The brief's own measured 250 is the correct one.

D. The one background step down the page has zero corpus support. All 11 web home feeds in the tally set sit on white and not one alternates grey and white bands. The tray under "New in Basel" is here because FLOORS LAW 4 asks for it and because the live page measures 95.5% white at 390, a named floor break. It is a Solen-specific choice, not a reference-backed one, and it is the first thing to cut if the page reads busy.

E. The card review count is gone, and CORPUS.md is stale on this. I first built the card with the rating value plus a parenthesised review count, because CORPUS.md section 2 records star plus value plus count as adopted and already shipped, 11 of 16 screens. The resurrection gate caught it: the converged card C11, CARD_REDESIGN_2026-07-13, supersedes that and puts the count on results pages only. Later dated decision wins, so the count is out. Worth flagging upward, because CORPUS.md was written 2026-07-29, two weeks AFTER the decision that overrides it, and still reads "Adopt, already shipped", so the next person building from that document will make the same mistake I did.
