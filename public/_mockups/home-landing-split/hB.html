The bet

One row. The search control never occupies vertical space on home: it collapses into a pill between the wordmark and the hamburger that shows the query as a summary, and the three real fields live in a sheet behind one tap. It answers the width complaint by not needing width, since a summary is something you READ, not three targets you have to hit.

Navigation answer, stated in full

Row one is the entire navigation. Wordmark on the left goes home. Collapsed pill in the middle is search. Hamburger on the right holds everything else: account, bookings, saved, language, and Solen for business, which is the route to the split-off landing page at /de/discover. Nothing is added to the chrome anywhere else on the page: no docked control at the foot of the screen, no tab strip, and no extra row beneath the header. The only other route off this screen is the one quiet blue text link in the footer, which duplicates the hamburger's business entry and exists because a link a first-time visitor can see beats one they have to open a menu to find.

The pill is three tap targets, not one. Each segment opens the sheet focused on its own field, which is exactly what the shipped SearchBar already does through its initialFocus prop, so this is a geometry change and not a new mechanism.

The measured numbers that drove it

Live on /de at 390x844 the gap between the wordmark and the hamburger is 243px, and the first salon card sits at y = 842 against an 844 fold, so a phone's first screen carries 0.00% photographic area. The 250px stacked search card is what costs that. Against it, every mobile entry control in the 24-app corpus is ONE row or absent: Airbnb 55pt, Square Go 51pt, Uber Eats 41pt, Fresha iOS 0pt. Not one of the 19 iOS home screens stacks three segments. This direction is that finding applied literally.

Does the summary actually fit? Measured with fontTools against the real Inter binary, not eyeballed. The pill lands at 219.1px wide, because the two 12px gaps spend 24 of the 243. Inside it, 163.1px is text area. The empty state needs 142.2px, so it FITS with 20.9px to spare. The filled state needs 254.9px and overflows by 91.8px, which is frame B.

What this file renders at: first salon photo top at y = 125, header 64px, pill 219px wide against the 243px that was available, 4 salon photos intersecting the fold, and 40.7% photographic area in the first viewport.

What it gives up

1. A tap before the query language exists. A first-time visitor in Basel never sees that Solen indexes by TIME until they open the sheet. That is the named cost of the collapsed pill in the corpus, and it lands hardest on us because we are pre-launch, so every visitor is a first-timer and none of them has a saved query the summary could display.

2. The summary truncates, and the overflow is 91.8px. Frame B shows it: the third field, the one that decides whether a booking is even possible, is the one that gets cut. Fresha iOS ships the identical truncation on its own summary pill. This is the direction's real weakness and it is worth rejecting on purpose rather than discovering later.

3. Two of the three icons leave the home screen. Only the magnifier survives above the fold. The map pin and the calendar exist only inside the sheet.

4. No room for a returning-user slot. The strongest returning-user pattern in the corpus is a state block directly under the entry control, held independently by Zocdoc, Airbnb and Fresha. This direction spends the whole top zone on one row, so an upcoming-appointment or book-again block would have to push the first salon card back down. That costs nothing today, because there is no booking history to show, and it costs real estate the day there is.

What it deliberately does not touch

The feed. Same horizontal rails, same real-size 231px cards, same three-row card, same 48px section rhythm. The only change on this page is the header, so the first-card number is attributable to the header alone and is directly comparable against the other directions.
