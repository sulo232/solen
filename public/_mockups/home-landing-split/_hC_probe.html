hC, search owns the bar, nav moves inside it

Navigation answer. There is no header row. The search control IS the header: one sticky 358 by 56 pill, and the logo mark and the hamburger are tenants INSIDE it, not walls around it. The wordmark shrinks to a 26px ink mark on the left. The hamburger stays on the right where he likes it, 44 by 44, and it is the ONLY navigation object on the screen: it opens the existing MobileMenu sheet holding account, bookings, saved, language and "Solen for business", which is the single link out to /de/discover. Nothing else navigates: no stacked chrome row, no tab strip, and nothing pinned along the bottom edge. Tapping any of the three segments opens the existing SearchOverlay at that segment, which is exactly what the real SearchBar already does (openOverlay(seg)), so there is no inline submit button: the ink commit lives in the overlay footer. Home carries no paid commit action, so the one-ink-CTA rule has no site on this screen.

The measured number that drove it. On the live /de at 390 by 844 the gap between the logo, which ends at x=87, and the hamburger, which starts at x=330, is 243px. A pill dropped into that gap is 227px wide, so the search occupies 58.2% of the row and reads as the smallest of three objects. That is the arithmetic behind "the search is too small", and he is right. Here the control is the full 358px bar: 91.8% of screen width. The honest caveat, stated rather than buried: this direction does NOT win on text pixels. One field in the sandwich pill gets about 173px of text run; here three fields share about 162px between them. The win is the RATIO, not the character count. The search stops looking small because it stops being an object inside a row and becomes the row.

What it gives up. First, the spelled-out wordmark, permanently. A 26px "S" is all the brand a first-time visitor sees above the fold, and in a market where nobody has heard of Solen that is a real cost, not a rounding error. Second, and this is the direction's real problem rather than a nuisance, the bar is legible only while it is empty. Measured on the render, the three text runs are 85.9, 55.1 and 55.1px. The empty placeholders fit easily at 50.1, 34.8 and 24.9px, which is the state drawn above and it looks fine. Filled, it stops fitting: canvas-measured at the real 14px semibold value weight, the time field clips on all four of the period labels its own picker offers, the shortest of which already measures 60px against a 55.1px run, the city field clips on Lausanne at 65.7 and Winterthur at 73.1 and on the live "current location" value at 123.4, and the service field clips on the longest entry in the live service list at 103.8 against an 85.9px run. So this bar shows a beautiful invitation and then truncates the answer, which is the exact opposite of the one rule every app in WHY_ENTRY agrees on, that the control shows the query when a query exists. If this direction is picked, that has to be solved, most likely by letting the filled bar collapse to a single summary line and give up the three-way split. Third, the inline submit. Nothing on home says "find appointments", so the commit sits one screen further away than it does today. Fourth, the scroll promise is a band, not a cropped card. The density floor asks for a visibly cropped next ITEM; what crops at y=800 is section 2's sunken tray. That is deliberate, and the reason is the next paragraph.

A finding, not a design choice: the mockup photo pool is 8 photographs, not 12. PIL-measured this session, MD5 plus a 64 by 64 perceptual diff over /_mockups/_assets/salon-photos: p04 and p05 are byte-identical, same MD5, and p01/p10, p02/p08, p09/p11 are the same shot re-encoded, at mean absolute difference 0.10, 0.18 and 0.16 on a 0 to 255 scale. So the folder README's "12 self-hosted product seed photos" is really 8. This page therefore carries 8 salon cards on 8 distinct photographs, zero reuse, and stops at three sections rather than the four the home-feed density floor asks for, because a fourth salon section would have forced the same photo under two business names. That is WHY_IT_LOOKS_CHEAP defect 2, which measured 19 slots against 11 photographs, reproduced inside the asset folder we build every mockup from. It needs more photography, not more layout.

Second half of the same finding: what the eight photographs are actually OF. I opened every one rather than trusting the filenames, and the first render had a card labelled Barber showing a tray of makeup brushes and a card labelled Nails showing a facial. Every label here is now set to what its own photograph shows: three hair, one barbershop, two makeup, one beauty flat lay, one facial. The pool contains no nails, no spa and no massage photography at all, so those three category tiles can be navigated to but cannot be illustrated by any card. A real label sitting over an unrelated photograph is the most recognisable placeholder tell there is, and it is worth naming that the constraint is the asset folder, not the design.
