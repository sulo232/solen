hA , "Stacked, Airbnb proper"

Airbnb does not sandwich its search between the logo and the menu, it stacks: a logo row, then a search row of its own beneath it. This is that, at 390.

1. The navigation answer

Two rows of chrome, and the hamburger is the only navigation. Row one (y 0 to 64) carries the wordmark left and the 44x44 hamburger right, which he said he likes. Row two (y 64 to 120) carries nothing but search, running the whole 358px between the 16px gutters. Nothing is docked to the bottom edge of this screen. Every destination that is not a salon lives behind the hamburger; every destination that is a salon is a card. There is no third kind of place to go, which is why one menu button is enough.

The search control is the real one re-geometried, not redrawn: the same three CollapsedRow segments, the real lucide search / map-pin / calendar glyphs, the real labels Service / Stadt / Zeit, the same white + elevation-2 + no-border container (edge-visibility route c), and the same ink commit control shrunk to a 44px round button. 250px of stacked card became one 56px row.

2. The measured number that drove it

His complaint is arithmetic and it is correct. On /de the logo ends at x=71 and the hamburger starts at x=330, so a sandwiched pill gets 243px. Stacked on its own row it measures 358px. That is +47.3%, bought for one 56px row.

The second number set the whole vertical budget. Today the first salon card sits at y=842, one pixel inside an 844 fold, and the phone first viewport measures 0.00% photographic. Deleting the h1 and its subline and collapsing the 250px search stack to 56 buys 708px back. Measured on this file at 390 wide:

  • first salon card top = y=134 (was 842)
  • card rows at 134 / 356 / 578 / 800
  • 6 full cards visible, plus 44px of row 4 cropped above the fold: the scroll promise
  • photographic area in the first 844px = 47.27% (floor is ~33%)
  • 3 distinct font sizes (24 / 14 / 12), ceiling is 4
  • 25% of first-viewport text at weight >= 600, ceiling is ~30%
  • smallest touch target 44px; 8 photo slots, 8 distinct scenes, 0 broken images

Two columns is not a style preference, it is the only shape that clears both floors at once. The locked 1.25 photo ratio makes a one-column card 358x286, so one column puts 1.5 cards in view and fails the >=4-unit density floor. A photo-left list row hits 4 units but drops photographic area to roughly 14% and fails the imagery floor. At 171 wide the photo is 137 tall and both floors clear.

3. What this direction gives up

  • 56px of every screen, forever. The row is the cost of the row, and nothing else on the page gets that space back.
  • The middle of the logo row is empty, a measured 269px of it. Sandwiching at least put something there. A resolved-city chip would fill it, but Stadt is already a search segment and stating the city twice is the duplication taste rule 2 exists to stop.
  • No display anchor >= 28px. With the promise headline deleted the largest text is the 24px wordmark, 1.71x body, under the 1.8x ratio floor. Legal only because FLOORS LAW 6 exempts a screen whose photograph is the focal, and at 47.27% it plainly is. Naming it rather than growing the wordmark to game a number.
  • The category taxonomy is now below the first viewport. A first-time visitor learns what Solen covers from the salons, not from a grid. Same trade Airbnb makes, opposite of the one Fresha iOS makes.
  • A magnifier renders twice in the search row, once as the Service field glyph and once in the ink button. Airbnb web and the real SearchBar both do this, but it is a redundancy and whether it reads as one is his call.
  • Smaller photos than the real rail card, 171 wide against 231. Density was bought with photo size.

Both defects passed every number and were only visible in the render. First, a UA body margin put the header at y=8 and shifted every row down with it. Second, and worse, the reset .hA-root button outranked .hA-go on specificity, so the one ink commit control computed rgba(0,0,0,0) and was invisible while every measurement still reported it 44x44 in the right place. Both fixed and re-verified.

The supply limit, which is not a design choice. A ninth card could not be added without repeating a scene, which is the same defect the live home page already has at nineteen slots to eleven photographs.

The bet

One row. The search control never occupies vertical space on home: it collapses into a pill between the wordmark and the hamburger that shows the query as a summary, and the three real fields live in a sheet behind one tap. It answers the width complaint by not needing width, since a summary is something you READ, not three targets you have to hit.

Navigation answer, stated in full

Row one is the entire navigation. Wordmark on the left goes home. Collapsed pill in the middle is search. Hamburger on the right holds everything else: account, bookings, saved, language, and Solen for business, which is the route to the split-off landing page at /de/discover. Nothing is added to the chrome anywhere else on the page: no docked control at the foot of the screen, no tab strip, and no extra row beneath the header. The only other route off this screen is the one quiet blue text link in the footer, which duplicates the hamburger's business entry and exists because a link a first-time visitor can see beats one they have to open a menu to find.

The pill is three tap targets, not one. Each segment opens the sheet focused on its own field, which is exactly what the shipped SearchBar already does through its initialFocus prop, so this is a geometry change and not a new mechanism.

The measured numbers that drove it

Live on /de at 390x844 the gap between the wordmark and the hamburger is 243px, and the first salon card sits at y = 842 against an 844 fold, so a phone's first screen carries 0.00% photographic area. The 250px stacked search card is what costs that. Against it, every mobile entry control in the 24-app corpus is ONE row or absent: Airbnb 55pt, Square Go 51pt, Uber Eats 41pt, Fresha iOS 0pt. Not one of the 19 iOS home screens stacks three segments. This direction is that finding applied literally.

Does the summary actually fit? Measured with fontTools against the real Inter binary, not eyeballed. The pill lands at 219.1px wide, because the two 12px gaps spend 24 of the 243. Inside it, 163.1px is text area. The empty state needs 142.2px, so it FITS with 20.9px to spare. The filled state needs 254.9px and overflows by 91.8px, which is frame B.

What this file renders at: first salon photo top at y = 125, header 64px, pill 219px wide against the 243px that was available, 4 salon photos intersecting the fold, and 40.7% photographic area in the first viewport.

What it gives up

1. A tap before the query language exists. A first-time visitor in Basel never sees that Solen indexes by TIME until they open the sheet. That is the named cost of the collapsed pill in the corpus, and it lands hardest on us because we are pre-launch, so every visitor is a first-timer and none of them has a saved query the summary could display.

2. The summary truncates, and the overflow is 91.8px. Frame B shows it: the third field, the one that decides whether a booking is even possible, is the one that gets cut. Fresha iOS ships the identical truncation on its own summary pill. This is the direction's real weakness and it is worth rejecting on purpose rather than discovering later.

3. Two of the three icons leave the home screen. Only the magnifier survives above the fold. The map pin and the calendar exist only inside the sheet.

4. No room for a returning-user slot. The strongest returning-user pattern in the corpus is a state block directly under the entry control, held independently by Zocdoc, Airbnb and Fresha. This direction spends the whole top zone on one row, so an upcoming-appointment or book-again block would have to push the first salon card back down. That costs nothing today, because there is no booking history to show, and it costs real estate the day there is.

What it deliberately does not touch

The feed. Same horizontal rails, same real-size 231px cards, same three-row card, same 48px section rhythm. The only change on this page is the header, so the first-card number is attributable to the header alone and is directly comparable against the other directions.
