Push Inspo's real photos up

Both boxes below are your real Inspo feed, loaded live, at its real phone size. The second one has one small change: the row of category buttons above the photos loses a little of its own empty space, so the real photos start higher.

What the first look actually shows. I was told this screen's first look measures 0.71% photographic, and I reproduced that number myself, on a fresh page load. But I also let the exact same screen keep loading, untouched, and once every real photo finished arriving it measured 65.41%, well past the 33% target. Both numbers are real. The gap between them is how long the real photos take to arrive, which was anywhere from about a second to nearly a minute while I tested it, depending on how busy the server was. That is a slow-photo problem, not an empty-screen problem, and it needs a different fix than this one. What this mockup fixes is separate and smaller: shrinking the chrome so whatever loads, loads a little higher and a little bigger.

What actually changed. One padding value, on the row of category buttons (All, Hair, Barber, Nails...) that already sits above your photo feed. It gets 8 pixels less air on top and 8 less on the bottom. The buttons, the search bar, the filter chips, the category photo row, the whole feed below, all identical. No photo was added, removed, or resized.
What this does not fix. This screen also has no real headline on phone right now, the biggest text on it is the 14px search placeholder. Pushing photos up does not touch that, it needs its own fix. And the slow-photo loading described above is a delivery-speed problem behind the scenes, not something a layout change can solve.
A separate small thing I found. When a category in that button row has no photo yet, it currently shows as a bare gray box, no icon, nothing. Every category on the site right now happens to have a photo, so this never shows today, but it is a real gap worth a cheap fix on its own.
Both boxes load the same real route, not a redraw. The percentages above come from measuring the real page directly (a script that reads every real photo on screen), at its real 390x844 phone size, not from eyeballing this preview.
