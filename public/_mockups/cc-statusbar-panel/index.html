What is real and what is not. Every number in the calm column was read off this machine at 16:12 today by the code that already ships: context and cost from the transcript parser, the two limits from ~/.claude.json, the subagent names and durations from this session's own metadata. The warning and error columns are the identical panel with the numbers moved past their thresholds, so you can judge the colour escalation without waiting to hit it. The spend sparkline is the one shape here drawn rather than measured, because per-turn history is not persisted yet; that is idea 9 on the list, and it only starts collecting the day it is switched on.

Why it reads this way. The step you are on is the biggest text in the panel because it is the thing you actually want at a glance. Limits sit under it as bars rather than numbers because a bar answers "how close am I" without you doing arithmetic. Warnings take the top slot and push everything down, because a warning you have to scroll to is not a warning. The subagent rows carry a name, an elapsed time and a cost, and nothing else. No tool names anywhere.

Measured, not eyeballed. Rendered at 1440x900 and measured: the anchor is 1.85x the body text (the range law floor is 1.8), four distinct type sizes (ceiling four), and 18% of the panel's text carries weight 600 or above (ceiling 30%). The first pass failed two of those at 1.54x and 46%, so the anchor was raised and the labels de-bolded until it passed.

Light on purpose. The real panel inherits your active VS Code theme through its --vscode-* variables, so it will match whatever you are using. This mockup is light because that is the house rule for anything rendered in a browser.
