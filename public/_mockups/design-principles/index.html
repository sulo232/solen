1. Semantic colour gets a legal and an illegal role
Green as WORDS measures 3.30 on white and 3.00 on this grey tray, under the 4.5 that text needs. So the green moves to the dot, which only needs 3.0, and the word goes ink. The meaning survives; the illegibility does not. 72 places currently use green as text.
2. Blue comes off the grey tray
Our accent blue is 4.58 on white, a pass, but 4.17 on the sunken tray, a fail. Our own layout rule pushes grouped content onto that exact tray. Blue stays on white; on grey the link keeps its affordance by underline instead. 7 elements are in that combination today.
3. Reading copy is 16, dense rows stay 14
We use one body size for two different jobs. 14 is right for dense rows, and Carbon and Atlassian agree. For text people actually read, Material defaults to 16, Apple floors at 17, and Carbon and Atlassian both step up to 16. The review paragraph is the thing that changes; the rows are untouched.
4. The anchor gets big enough to be an anchor
The heading goes 22 to 28, clearing the 28px floor. Apple and Material land on 28 independently for this tier, which is the strongest evidence available: two competing systems, same number, arrived at separately.
5. The row divider is inset
The rule between rows now starts and stops inside the tray. A line that reaches both sides cuts the container in half instead of separating the rows within it.
6. The size count comes down
Measured on the live home page at phone width: 7 distinct sizes against a ceiling of 4, and 4 weights against a ceiling of 2. Not a new rule, an existing one that has drifted. The counters above read the rendered page live, so they cannot claim a number the screen is not actually hitting.
Two things this deliberately does NOT change
The star stays yellow at 1.60, below the guideline, because every rating anyone has ever seen is a yellow star. My lean is a thin outline rather than a new colour, and that one is yours. And the ink commit button stays ink: the rule against blue on a big button turned out to be a minority position rather than the industry norm it claimed to be, but it is a position you picked deliberately, so it stands until you say otherwise.
