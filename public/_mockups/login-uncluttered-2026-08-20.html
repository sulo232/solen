What came off, and why each one counted as clutter
  • The menu button. A second way out of a screen whose only job is getting you in. Your own nav rule says one control per sub-page.
  • The subtitle. It explains what signing in is, to someone who already tapped sign in.
  • The word "or" with two rules through it. The empty gap already says it, which is exactly what the reference does.
  • The legal line. That belongs where an account gets created, not on a return visit.
  • The blue. Forgot password becomes an underlined ink link, so the one strong thing on the screen is the button you actually want pressed.
Removing those is not only taste. It fixes two things your own rules already forbid. Measured on the live screen: 5 different text sizes and 3 different weights in one view, where your floor is 4 and 2. Both proposals come in at 2 and 2.
One thing in the reference collides with a decision you made, so I did not just apply it. Quizlet's fields are grey filled with no outline. On 2026-08-09 you said inputs are white with a thin line, and that tapping one should change nothing visible. B uses the grey fill so you can see it against A. If you prefer B, that is you changing that call, not me quietly dropping it.
The other thing I did not copy. The reference has three ways in, so its bottom block reads as a set of choices. You have two, and right now they match on all five things I compared: width, height, corner radius, fill and border. They read as one button repeated. B gives them different fills. A keeps them identical and just moves them down.
