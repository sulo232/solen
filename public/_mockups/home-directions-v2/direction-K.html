THE BET. Home's job is to make your last appointment repeatable in one tap. In beauty you come back to a person on a four to eight week cycle, so the screen opens on the return and browsing is what happens when the answer is no. Everything above the tray serves one sentence: same person, same service, one tap. The search row is the exit for the people we failed, the rail is the plural of the anchor, the feed is third because it is the fallback. That ordering IS the argument. Read top to bottom, this screen says the product is a relationship you resume, not a catalogue you shop.

WHAT IT GIVES UP. First, imagery in the first viewport. Arithmetic on the CSS boxes in this file, not a rendered measurement, and I did not open a browser: at 390x844 the visible photographic area is two 173x138 card photos plus three 48px thumbs, 54,798 of 329,160 px, 16.6 percent against the FLOORS LAW 2 target of about a third. That is a named violation, not an oversight. It is the bill for spending the top of the screen on text, and the feed's own viewport one scroll down runs well above the floor. Second, the category row: K deletes MobileCategoriesRow from position one and does not re-add it anywhere. What survives in its place is the search row plus a six-card feed, so nothing that row did is lost, only its position. Third, it pays nothing at launch. Solen has no booking history and fabricating one is banned, so a first-timer sees this screen with the anchor and the rail gone, which is strictly worse than today unless the rail backfills real top-rated salons the way RecentlyViewed.tsx already does at line 134. Fourth, the rail needs a read that does not exist: useRecentlyViewed.ts returns VIEWED salons, and this rail needs BOOKED ones with the service, the amount paid and the date, plus a minimum count RecentlyViewed.tsx does not have (line 110 renders at one entry, which is Vrbo's one-card hole). Fifth, this rail row is SalonCard's second anatomy, so it is legal only as a documented variant, never as a second implementation, and that question has to be settled before the row is designed rather than after. Sixth, promoting rebooking to home needs an explicit owner yes, because a barber-era express-rebook widget sits in the graveyard from July 2026, and a narrow reason for deleting something is still not permission to bring it back.

TWO THINGS I CHECKED AND FOUND DIFFERENT. The research this direction came from says express-rebook has zero customer render site. That is false: ProfileTabs.tsx calls POST /api/bookings/express-rebook at line 121 and the confirm endpoint at line 138, today, in the customer app. Rebooking already ships. It is just one tab deep inside the profile, which makes K's claim narrower and stronger, move the action we already built to the screen people actually open. The same research says the next-free line is unwired. Also false: GET /api/slots/next-available exists and reads availability_slots for the next slot with status available. What it filters on is the salon, not the stylist, so "Next free Thursday" is honest at salon level today and needs one more filter to be honest about Nadia specifically. Both corrections are in the file header with line numbers.

THE LENS FINDING BEHIND IT. Fresha is the only app in the sweep that promotes rebooking to the home screen, first section, directly under the greeting and above Favourites. Angi instead gave return its own tab called Pros, wrote the reason on screen ("We know how valuable it is to find a pro you trust"), and then had to build a full-screen illustrated explainer to teach people the tab exists, which is the measured invoice for hiding it. Square Go's no-upcoming state IS a past visit plus a full-width Rebook. Zocdoc asks it outright as a two-card fork, rebook a previous provider or book a new one. Starbucks' Your Usuals row is its entire returning-user proposition. Airtasker's "Use details from last task with Usama" is the most complete version found. The contrast that makes the bet: Airbnb resumes a SEARCH, "Continue searching for experiences in Miami", and never says book that place again, because its user returns to a category while ours returns to a person. The structural move here, stripping the photograph out of the personal block, is not mine either: Marriott renders CONTINUE YOUR BOOKING as text with a left thumbnail, Fresha demotes Book again to a thumb row above photo-top Favourites. That is the corpus's own answer to the collision between a pinned personal block and a photo-led feed, and it is why the loudest thing on this screen is a person's name in 28px type rather than a room.
