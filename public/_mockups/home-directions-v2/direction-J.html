Target state. Photos are the seeded set standing in for salon-uploaded work: today salon_portfolio_images holds 0 rows, so no tile above is wired to a real portfolio. Names, neighbourhoods, prices and star values are illustrative, not a live query.

The bet. Home's job is to show finished work, because you cannot judge a stranger but you can judge their output. The photograph is the product and the salon drops to a credit line under it. The whole screen is one declaration: the tile title says what the work IS, at 14px weight 600, and the business that made it sits beneath at 12px weight 400 in grey. Every other direction inverts exactly those two values.

What it gives up. A content-operations commitment before it is a design decision, and the seeded set lets me price that instead of asserting it: of 8 distinct photos, one is a genuinely finished result (the colour tile), two are treatments mid-work with a practitioner's hands in frame, two are product flat-lays, one is an empty interior, one is a team portrait, and nails has zero, which is why two tiles here are honest fallbacks. Result photos are also not comparable: two beautiful pictures cannot tell you which salon is nearer, cheaper or sooner, so home becomes a browsing surface and the entire deciding job moves to search. And the photo is a seller-selected best case with no way to constrain it, unlike a price or a slot. Two costs I found while building that the lenses had not priced: the feed's unit stops being the salon, so tiles one and seven are one business and tiles two and five are another, which makes 8 tiles only 5 businesses and means a salon with 40 photos would flood the feed without a per-salon cap; and a work stream is one stream, which breaks the "home needs at least 4 sections" density floor outright. The chip row is doing the sectioning instead.

The lens finding behind it. Direction J came from WHY_CURRENCY position 3 carrying its own caveat, that no beauty-booking product in the corpus leads with result photography. I searched to break that and could not: Fresha's nail list is text rows with a monogram disc and no photo at all, Careem demotes the manicure shot to a 60px left thumbnail subordinate to the price, and Fresha puts Photos in a separate tab so the bookable list stays text-only. So the direction still rests on a structural analogy, and the analogy is strong where I could check it. Artsy is the closest full instance: the artwork is the photo, then the maker, then the work's own title, then the gallery as a grey credit line, then the price, in a market whose premise is that you cannot audit a reputation but you can look at the picture. Airtasker is the only card that refuses the person-versus-work choice, a photo of a finished eyebrow above the tasker's name and rating, and it quietly proves this direction's limit by inverting its own card for search results into a left-thumbnail list: work-photo-large to browse, work-photo-small to decide. Behance and ZIGZAG both put a small avatar credit directly under the work and carry no price slot at all. Airbnb Services photographs the work rather than a venue, titles every listing "by Putu", and makes its category tiles work photos with real supply counts, printing "1 available" rather than hiding thinness. Pinterest settles the Inspo collision inside one app: Explore is a bare masonry with no text, Shop is the same grid plus a price and a merchant credit, so a price is what converts an inspiration wall into a bookable one. Pinterest and GOAT overlay that price as a pill on the image; Depop, Etsy, eBay and Faire put it in a line below. Solen's lock picks the second, body weight in the meta line, never a second anchor.

What building it turned up. This direction is not speculative: the owner already commissioned its data model on 2026-07-25 ("portfolio to be categories, like men's cut or a woman's cut, that the salon itself can upload those stuff and categorize it"), and salon_portfolio_images, the 15-key taxonomy, the gallery API and the dashboard uploader are all live. It holds 0 rows and has no customer browse surface, so J is the missing consumer of a built pipeline. Two real seams: the taxonomy has no makeup key across its four verticals, so the Styling tile above is a judgement call and makeup work is currently uncategorizable; and a portfolio row carries a category but no service and no price, so pairing "Color" with "from 165 CHF" on one tile is a join that does not exist yet, which matters because PBV Art. 13 needs the price attached to the named service. Also worth fixing separately: the salon-photos README claims 12 photos, and hashing all 66 pairs shows 4 pairs identical, so it is 8.

The question it forces. Not whether to adopt this, but whether Inspo is a tab or the front door. A two-column masonry of hair photos with prices IS Inspo, and Pinterest's answer is that you do not build a second grid, you add a price to the one you have.
