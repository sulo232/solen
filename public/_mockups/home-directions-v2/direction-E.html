Direction E. The query, not the catalogue. Home's job is to get three answers out of you (what, where, when) before it shows you anything, because a salon list built on a guess is a promise we then have to take back. The screen is a form, then the answer to that form. Read top to bottom it is one sentence: you said Coiffeur, in Zurich, on Saturday, so here are the 24 salons that can actually take you.

What it gives up. It re-asks a returning customer three questions it already knows the answers to; that cost is permanent and unfixable inside this bet. It makes home a poor browsing surface by design, so all merchandising happens after a search. It deletes the mobile category grid from home (MobileCategoriesRow, currently the first thing under the hero at page.tsx:200) and folds the taxonomy into the Service field, which is exactly Fresha web's mechanism and exactly its named cost: somebody who does not know the word Barbershop now has to open a field to find out barbershops exist. It drops the category tag from every result, because the Service field already declared the category and a tag repeating it is meta that adds no decision (copy economy rule 4). It cuts the save-heart and the account button, which serve browsing and account, not this screen's job. And it cannot go all the way to Fresha's version: Fresha's inventory-free hero is legible only because a wired counter sits under it, and pre-launch Solen has no number it can print honestly, so the form shares the first viewport with real salons instead of owning it.

What it does NOT give up, and what paid for that. The direction was written expecting to sacrifice the first salon photo above the fold permanently. It does not have to. Two cuts buy it back: the h1 shrinks to one line, and City and When pair side by side on one row instead of stacking, on Trip.com's from/to row and Skyscanner's two-date row. The form measures 200 px for three fields where SearchBar.tsx:100 spends 250, so the first salon is complete at 703 px and the second one's photo starts at 715 and is cropped at the 844 fold with 129 px of it showing, which is the scroll promise. The date is the one answer the results cannot show, which is why the quiet count line restates only the date and not the whole tuple.

The lens findings behind it. From WHY_ENTRY position 1 and bet E1: Fresha web's four-segment bar whose treatment dropdown IS the category list and which carries the filled query into the results header as a state receipt; Airbnb web, whose bar changes arity with its own state; and Airbnb Experiences shipping three segments not four, because an experience is a point in time and a stay is a range, which is why the Zeit segment is already the right shape and no fourth field is argued for. New this pass, because the entry corpus contains no phone that gives search more than one row: the population that does hold this bet on a phone is travel, and it resolves the height three different ways. Booking.com and BlaBlaCar flush-stack the fields inside one hairline-divided container with the submit filling the bottom edge, which says the fields are one statement; Vrbo gap-separates them into three outlined pills and pays for it with dead space; Trip.com and Skyscanner pair two fields per row to halve the row count. This screen takes Booking.com's flush stack and Trip.com's pairing, and keeps Vrbo's label-above-value so each row states both its question and its answer. Booking.com also ships its home fields filled rather than as placeholders, which is why this screen shows a live tuple: the form is the state receipt, not a blank to complete. Separately, four apps that show a query and inventory in one phone viewport (Realtor.com, Zillow, Redfin, Trip.com Discover) all print a result count and all print it quiet; every count across ten web captures was small too, so the count here is 12 px and is deliberately not the anchor. Peerspace is the closest analogue found in the sweep: one field, a chip rail, then real venue photos with a from-price badge.

measured: Mobbin iOS captures render at 299 px for a ~390 pt device, 1.304 pt/px (method: research/AXIS_GRID.md section 0), PIL-measured this session. Booking.com Stays form container = 275 px wide = 359 pt, 146 px tall = 190 pt, three filled value rows plus a flush full-width submit. This screen's form is 358 px at a 390 px width, within 1 pt of it, and 198 px tall, the extra going to the submit's 12 px inset because the design contract locks buttons to a pill and Booking.com's is a flush rectangle. BlaBlaCar = 263 px wide = 343 pt, 300 pt tall for four rows plus a 56 pt submit whose x-range equals the container's exactly, so flush is confirmed rather than assumed; its three-field equivalent works out at 239 pt against this screen's 200 px, which is what the side-by-side pair is worth. Vrbo leaves 467 pt (358 px) of trapped white between its last field and its Search button, which is the dead-space failure measured, and is why nothing here gaps the rows. One mistake worth recording, because it is the failure mode this estate keeps having. I first drew the discount badge as a rose pill over the photo, because that is literally what SalonCard.tsx:238 and :241 do. A gate stopped it: that DiscountBadge is dormant V3-D85 code that never renders live, and the owner rejected the rose photo tag twice, on mobile and again on sight in the 2026-07-15 taste lab. The current decision is a pale-green pill beside the price, which is what ships here. Source code is not proof a treatment is current, and I needed the gate to tell me that rather than the other way round.

One thing the screenshot caught that the markup did not. The first render put the same photo on two salons twice over. The seeded pool at _mockups/_assets/salon-photos holds twelve files but only about six visually distinct images: p04 and p05 are byte-identical, and p01/p10, p02/p08 and p09/p11 are the same photo re-cropped, which no hash check catches. This screen now uses the six distinct ones plus the fallback for the seventh salon. Two of the six are beauty rather than hair, which is a limit of the pool, not a claim about what a Coiffeur search should return.
