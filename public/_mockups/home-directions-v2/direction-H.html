Direction H. The next free day.

The bet. Home's job is to turn "I need a haircut this week" into a held appointment, so the screen is organised on one axis, when, in two places: a rail that sets the earliest day you are willing to go, and one row per card naming that salon's own soonest bookable day. The feed is sorted soonest first, which is why the day words run down the page as a column: today, today, tomorrow, tomorrow, Sat, Mon. That column is the argument. Nothing here ranks salons by anything except how soon you can sit down.

What it gives up

  • Card count, and there is no way around it. Measured on the render at 390x844, not estimated: each card is 328px tall, the feed starts at y=226, and the first viewport holds one complete card plus the whole photo of the second, about 1.6 content units against a density floor of 4 on mobile. It cannot be tuned away: the imagery lock makes the photo the largest element, so the day row's 44px cannot come out of the photo the way Tock pays it. It comes out of count. Confirmed rather than assumed, Zocdoc's card carries a week of day tiles and fits one card per viewport.
  • Browsing. A screen that asks "when" has assumed you already know "what". Anyone arriving curious rather than committed is served worse here than by any other direction, and the search pill is their only exit.
  • The category grid, dropped by name. It is first on the live home today (page.tsx:200), and "now" is the one thing a grid structurally cannot express. Keeping both puts zero inventory in the first viewport.
  • An owner-approved deletion, re-opened. CARD_REDESIGN_2026-07-13 C2 removed the next-slot row from this exact card. H puts it back, so this is an ask with a mockup, never a build. The seam survived: nextSlotLabel is still in SalonCardProps at SalonCard.tsx:302, kept unused by render.
  • A calm inspiration surface, permanently. Once the sort is availability, a salon with a beautiful room and a three-week wait ranks below a mediocre one with a gap tomorrow. H is honest about that. It is a screen for people in a hurry.

The lens finding behind it

WHY_RELATIONS F1 says a bookable time on a card forces either the photo to shrink or the count to halve, and I4 says no card in ten targeted searches carries photo, price and time as peers. So H does not try. It splits the currency across two levels, the way Square Go does: the week lives in the rail, one day lives on the card, and the price stays quiet in the meta row where PBV needs it and nowhere louder.

I checked the two claims this rests on and one was described to me wrongly. Zocdoc's day tiles with counts are not at the top of its screen, they are inside each card, and its unavailable days collapse into a single grey tile reading "Today to Tomorrow / No appts". That per-card version is closed to us: a circular avatar can afford a tile strip, a photo-dominant card cannot. The screen-level position used here is Superpower's, whose tiles read "10 slots", "86 slots", "103 slots". Its counts swing by ten times, which is the honest thin case, so Sunday here prints "closed" rather than disappearing.

The sharpest finding, and it went in H's favour. Every app in the corpus makes availability loud with a coloured fill: Zocdoc yellow tiles, Fresha and Resy filled chips, Careem a green disc. Solen banned that by name twice ("availability = plain ink text, NO green pill", plus the C2 deletion). So H had to buy loudness without colour, and the answer is better than a pill: a full-width row under a hairline, in the last position, repeated down the feed. It also leaves the card's one badge slot free, which a pill would have spent, so a discount pill can still coexist.

Three things found in the code, not assumed

1. The graveyard already holds this direction's exact failure. The barber StaffAvailability strip was re-deleted on 2026-07-14 because an auth-gate on /api/staff/[id]/availability turned an anonymous availability read into a fabricated "closed all week". H is an anonymous availability read on the home page, so that is the risk. Verified: /api/availability/[salon_id] has no auth check (no getUser, no session, no 401) and reads availability_slots, a real table with 9365 rows. Not verified: the RLS policy on that table, which is what actually decides whether a logged-out visitor sees slots. That one policy is the go or no-go for H and it needs checking before anyone builds this.

2. Two elements here are net-new and this names which. The per-card day and the "N open" count both come straight from /api/availability/[salon_id], which already returns available slots grouped by date. The cross-salon count in the rail ("11 free") exists nowhere: every availability endpoint is per-salon, so the rail needs a fan-out or a materialised view. "Soonest first" is also net-new as a sort. Running exists on "salon sort" returns nothing, and /api/salons takes a date that hard-filters availability rather than ranking by it.

3. The precedent for the day row already ships, at app/[locale]/dev/map-extras/page.tsx:46, rendering "Available today" with the comment "day granularity only, never a clock time on a card". It uses a neutral sunken pill and a clock icon. This uses a plain row and a calendar icon instead, because a clock icon on a label that never states a time invites the reader to look for one. Separately: the live missing-photo fallback at SalonCard.tsx:456-464 renders the initial only, with no category icon, so card 6 above is one element richer than what ships today.

Two collisions surfaced rather than quietly resolved

No day is pre-selected as a hard filter. If the rail filtered to a single day, all six cards would read the same word and the per-card row would carry no information, which is the trap this bet has to avoid. So the rail selects the earliest day you can go and the feed runs from there. That is a behavioural decision inside the bet, and it is yours to make.

V3-D442 says the name must be the larger of a card's two ink elements, while the bet wants the day to be the loudest thing on the card. Both hold here only because loudness is bought with position: name 16, day 14, and the row carries no tint. If you want the day genuinely bigger than the name, that is an amendment to V3-D442 and should be decided as one.

Every floor was checked against the rendered page rather than asserted. 4 distinct font sizes (28 / 16 / 14 / 12) and 2 weights (400 / 600), inside the type budget. 22.2% of first-viewport text at weight 600 (8 of 36), under the 30% ceiling. Anchor 28px, exactly 2.0x the 14px body, clearing both the 28px floor and the 1.8x ratio. Photographic area of the first viewport 43.5%, above the one-third imagery floor. Smallest touch target 44px. Both self-hosted fonts actually load, and only two families resolve anywhere on the page. The 57-character salon name truncates to one line (282px box against a 440px string) with the price row intact, so the worst-case content check passes.

Measuring caught two defects that reading the code would not have. documentElement.scrollWidth was 398, an 8px horizontal scroll at exactly the design width, caused by the host's default body margin; zeroed. And the search pill's label was computing to Arial, because a button takes the UA font instead of inheriting, so the one piece of copy in the top zone was in the wrong typeface; fixed with font-family: inherit. Both are in the file with a comment naming the measurement.

Not determined

Static, so no motion claim, and nothing here is verified on real iOS Safari, only Chromium at 390x844. The RLS policy on availability_slots is unchecked and is the real go or no-go. Whether "1 open" reads as honest scarcity or as pressure is not something a still can settle, and it is the first element to cut if it reads wrong.
