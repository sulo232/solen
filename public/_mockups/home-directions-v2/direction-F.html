The bet

Home's job is to prove, in the first screen, that there are enough real salons near you to make this worth using. So the content is actual salon cards plus honest per-category counts, and the search control shrinks to one row. It is the direct inverse of direction E: E says show nothing until the query is legal, F says the screen is worthless until it shows real supply.

What it gives up

  • The calm, and the single unambiguous action. The shipped mobile search control is 250px of three stacked segments plus an ink button (SearchBar.tsx:100). This direction cuts it to a 44px row. Anyone who arrives knowing exactly what they want is now served slightly worse so that everyone who does not is served much better.
  • Editorial and inspiration. Salon-of-the-month, the Inspo band and reviews all move below everything on this page. There is no room above the fold and F does not pretend there is.
  • Disclosure control. Real counts mean home publishes how few salons each Swiss category has. This page prints its smallest category and its smallest city in the first screen rather than hiding them. That is the forced consequence of the device and it is a business decision about disclosure, not a design decision, so it is yours (WHY_RELATIONS F5).
  • Name comparability at the edges. Two columns costs 173px of card width, so the longest Swiss compound name truncates (card 6). Per WHY_RELATIONS F2, raising the desktop rail from four cards to the six our own density floor asks for makes this worse, so FLOORS LAW 1(f) has to be re-run at the new column count before the count changes, not after.
  • The card's shipped row order. At 173px, a name and a rating on one baseline leaves the name about 16 characters, so the rating moved down a row and the named from-price took a row of its own. Measured reason, named in the file header, not a preference.

The lens finding behind it

Three things, and the second is the one I would defend hardest.

First, the count device is real and it always prints the thin case. Looked at as images this session, not recalled: Airbnb Services iOS puts the city in its biggest text ("Services in New York City"), then a rail of category tiles each carrying a label and a smaller grey supply line under it, and the smallest of those lines reads "1 available" rather than being suppressed. Wolt uses the identical anatomy on a photo tile ("Burger" then a grey places count under it) and also prints its smallest numbers. Two further devices show up in the same sweep, and both are query-responsive rather than decorative: Fresha's search puts a venue count on its own grey row between the query and the cards, and the same screen with a broader query shows a larger one, so the number is measuring something; Swiggy puts it in the section header instead, and DoorDash gives it a row of its own beside a Reset control. And OpenTable's web home is the shape this page borrows: a hero about a sixth of the viewport high, then immediately five restaurant cards and a cropped sixth, with a demand line printed on the thin rows as well as the busy ones, and grey no-photo placeholders left in place rather than the venue being hidden.

Second, and this is the argument I did not expect to find: F is the only direction that pays the imagery floor the way the owner said it must be paid. The floor asks for roughly a third of the viewport to be photographic, and the 2026-07-25 rejection says in the owner's own words that it is met by showing off the stores we have and never by an image baked into a layout. Every other way of hitting a third of the viewport with photography involves making something bigger. F hits it by making the count higher, so the photographic area on this page is entirely salons' own cover shots. Direction F does not merely comply with that floor. It is what that floor was asking for.

Third, the provenance device this page adds, which is not copied from anything above: the four category counts sum to the anchor. WHY_RELATIONS T1 lists four ways a number earns belief (unrounded precision, a freshness stamp, a named source, a stated window), and all four ask the reader to trust something off-screen. A breakdown that adds up to its own total can be audited without leaving the screen, which is strictly better, and it is why the two non-category tiles the shipped component carries (Karte, Walk-in) are dropped here: a walk-in count would double-count barbershops and break the sum.

One correction to my own reasoning, since it changes the price of this direction

I first wrote that the supply count was a net-new backend dependency, on the strength of npm run exists "supply count" returning nothing. Then I opened the endpoint. It already selects with an exact count and already returns a total beside its items (app/api/salons/route.ts:157 and :640), and its computed-filter path deliberately constrains before paginating so that count stays correct under a distance filter (:337). So the zero was a naming result, not a capability result, and this direction is much cheaper than I first said: one call per category, one per city, and a render site. I had it wrong in the expensive direction and it is worth correcting because it changes whether this is a project or an afternoon.

What I could not determine

Whether any of this converts: Mobbin is stills, so every claim above is a mechanism argument and none is a measured outcome. Whether Solen's real seeded photos actually discriminate between venues, which is the single unverified assumption the whole direction rests on, because if the photos do not distinguish one salon from another then more of them is more area and not more evidence (WHY_CURRENCY 5c, still open). And whether the counts are large enough to be worth printing at all: the anchor here is illustrative, and if the real Zurich number turns out to be small then this direction's own headline argues against using it, which is the honest test to run first, and it is now a one-line query rather than a build.
