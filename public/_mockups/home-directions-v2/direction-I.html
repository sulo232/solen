Direction I, the person, not the premises

THE BET. Home's job is to introduce the people who will actually cut your hair. So the photo slot holds the stylist, the title is her name, and the salon is the phrase after it. Mechanically this is the shipped SalonCard with rows 1 and 2 swapped and the photo re-pointed from the premises to the person. Nothing else about the card grammar moves: same three-row stack, same 14 / 12 / 12 sizes, same ink then ink-2 colour step, same from-price with the service named for PBV Art. 13, same ban on any time or date on a card.

The reason to bet this way is that a haircut is not a room you sit in. It is a decision a specific pair of hands makes about your face for the next six weeks. Every field on the shipped card describes a building. None of them describes the only variable that changes the outcome.

WHAT IT GIVES UP, in order of how much it costs

1. Density, and not on the axis everyone assumes. A face below thumbnail size stops being a face and silently becomes a row identifier, so the tile has to stay at 172px, which forces 2 across. The first-viewport floor still passes (four whole cards plus a cropped row), so the cost is NOT where it was predicted. It lands on desktop, where this grid cannot reach the 6-column floor at portrait scale without dropping to a 3-across rail, and on cards per scroll, which roughly halves. Skillshare and Care.com both run 6 across on web at about 110px, which is the honest ceiling for a face.

2. Photography we do not control, and this is the one that can kill it. Three of six tiles here carry no face, and that ratio is a placeholder, not a measurement. Fresha's own professional picker ships four tiles and zero faces: a dark disc reading "A", two lilac discs reading "TK" and "EL", with a plain job-title line carrying all the meaning instead. Braintrust ships "M" and "RM" in the middle of a real browse grid; Skillshare ships a wordmark tile among the portraits. So the fallback is the norm in this market rather than an edge case. If most seeded stylists have no avatar_url, this direction degrades into a grid of initials, which is a worse home screen than the one shipping today.

3. A venue-shaped product, in three places at once. The data model, the URL and the supply pitch to salon owners are all venue-first. Every card here still links through /salon/[slug]/staff/[staffId], so the person remains a child of the place. And the price this direction is legally required to show is not in /api/staff/featured at all, so a per-salon minimum-service join has to be built before this screen can render lawfully.

4. Comparability. Once the card leads with a person, the fields that let you rank two options (place, category, price) all shrink to 12px meta. This becomes a good screen for meeting people and a worse one for deciding between them. Open's Teachers page is the end of that road: sixteen beautiful studio portraits and not one piece of commercial information.

5. Fairness, and this is the objection I would take most seriously. A face-forward grid invites appearance-based selection in a market where that is a legal exposure, not only a taste question. You already ruled next to this on 28 July 2026, killing the junior / senior / master tier badge: "theyre just gonna choose, thats gonna be unfair". This file honours that literally, no tier or seniority label anywhere, and a service instead of a rank. But the deeper reading of that sentence is an argument against the whole direction, not only against the badge, and it deserves an answer rather than a workaround.

THE LENS FINDING BEHIND IT

Square Go's PDP title is literally "Monica Andrea at Salon Cozzolino", the person first and the salon as a prepositional phrase, and its own search rows are mostly people's names. Fresha's "Venues nearby" row carries Pam Zink, a person, with her face in the photo slot beside salon interiors, inside the same card component, which means that in this market a solo practitioner already IS a venue and the schema is pretending otherwise. Wonder's Browse by Chef is the closest match to this bet's exact text order: the chef's face, the chef's name as the title, then the restaurant demoted to a grey line. Care.com puts the face at full card geometry with "Background Checked" and "Hired by 2 neighbors" and lets the price go grey. Bloom and Tonal both label a practitioner by temperament ("Direct & Empowering", "High-Energy and Engaging") rather than by credential, which is the same instinct your tier-badge decision arrived at independently. Skillshare and Care.com prove the combination this bet needs actually ships: portrait scale and a price on the same tile. And the counter-evidence belongs inside the bet rather than outside it: Careem's staff step pre-selects "Auto assign, we'll assign the best professional" ahead of two named, photographed, rated staff, so the strongest product in this space believes its user does not want to choose a person at all.

Which is the sharpest thing to say about this direction: the pattern is proven in adjacent human-service markets (teaching, childcare, contract work, wellness) and actively declined by both beauty products we can see. That is not a reason to reject it. It is a reason to know exactly which bet you are making.

WHAT THIS SCREEN DROPS THAT OTHER DIRECTIONS KEEP, and why

No category shortcut row: a category is a property of a place, and this screen is about people. No save heart on the cards: a frost disc over a portrait covers the one thing the card exists to show, and the card is a single tap target so nothing inside it competes. No city line on the cards: the search control already says Zurich, so repeating it is padding. No section header above the grid: the 28px headline is the section title. No superlative title, because /api/staff/featured ranks by created_at desc today and has no featured-score data, so "top" or "best" would be a fabricated claim. No bottom tab bar drawn, which is a limit of the fragment and is measured in the header comment rather than hidden.

THREE THINGS FOR YOU, not decided here

The measurement that decides this direction is the avatar_url fill rate on the real seeded staff_members rows. High, and this is the strongest direction in the set. Low, and it is the weakest, and no amount of layout fixes that. I did not run it, so the 50/50 split drawn above is an assumption wearing a picture.

The backend already agrees with this bet, which surprised me. /api/bookings/express-rebook selects bookings.staff_member_id, filters the next opening with .eq("staff_member_id", ...) and returns staffName to the client. It already hunts for your next slot with the same stylist and already hands the interface her name. The server decided the person is the unit of return; only the UI never said so. The "Your stylist" row above is not a new feature, it is the same two-call pattern ProfileTabs.tsx:121 already runs, moved onto home. It still needs your explicit yes, because a barber-side one-tap rebook widget was deleted on 11 July 2026 and a narrow deletion reason is not permission.

FeaturedStylists is not dead, it is unplugged. The component exists, 298 lines, its anatomy was designed against a real reference on 25 May 2026, and it was pulled on 5 June 2026 because its cards pointed at /stylist/[slug], a route that does not exist. Its own header writes down the fix. So Direction I's claim is narrower than it looks: that component had the right entity and the wrong geometry, a 72px identifier inside a scan-and-compare list, and its own comment says so ("this is a SCAN/COMPARE pattern not a discovery pattern"). A person only becomes the product at portrait scale.
