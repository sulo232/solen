Two questions about every input on the site

Both are live contradictions, not new ideas. Click into the fields; they behave exactly as the real ones would. Answer in one line, for example "fill gray, focus edge".

1

The fill: gray or white?

Right now your booking lookup form renders BOTH, on the same screen. The order-number field is white with a hairline and a 16px radius; the email field right below it is filled gray with an invisible border and a 12px radius. Nobody chose that: a rule in the stylesheet targets inputs by their type, so a field with type="email" gets the gray fill while a field without a type keeps what its component asked for. Pick one and every form on the site matches.

a, filled grayYour own depth rule from 2026-06-09: a filled field, no hairline, going white when you focus it. This is what most typed inputs render today.
b, white with a hairlineWhat the components ask for in their own code today, and what the order-number field actually shows.
Measured live on /de/booking/lookup this turn: lookup-code = white, border #E4E4E7, radius 16. lookup-email = fill #F4F4F5, transparent border, radius 12. Same form, same height (52px), two different looks.
Whichever you pick, the loser's code gets deleted rather than left to fight: today the components write classes that the stylesheet silently overrides, so what you read in the code is not what renders.
2

The focus: edge only, or edge plus a soft glow?

Your design contract says an input gets one ink edge plus a single soft glow. Your own no-focus-ring rule refuses every glow, anywhere. Both cannot be true, and until today neither was: the focus law sat in a place where the styling never applied, so inputs had no focus mark at all. Click both fields and pick the one that is right.

a, ink edge only (shipping now)The border darkens to ink, the field goes white. Nothing else.
b, ink edge + soft glow (what the contract says)The same edge, plus a 3px ink glow at 10 percent.
Both clear the accessibility floor: the edge alone is roughly a 15:1 change against the resting state. This is taste, not compliance. On phones neither shows: you settled that on 2026-07-01 (the caret and keyboard already say where focus is).
