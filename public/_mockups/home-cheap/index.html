Diagnosis, 30 July 2026. Measured, not proposed.

Why it looks cheap

Two literal placeholders are shipped on this page: a 576 x 576 grey box carrying the universal image-failed-to-load icon (38.9% of a phone viewport, live for 65 days), and nineteen salon photo slots filled by eleven photographs, so the same interior appears under three different business names on one screen.

Everything else that reads cheap is one defect repeated on four axes: the page never spends a step to say that two things are different. The card's price, address and category render at identical size, weight and colour. The gap that binds a photo to its own caption is the same 8px as the gap that separates two unrelated blocks. The largest, boldest, only saturated text in the whole feed is a wait estimate, not a salon name.

The layout is mostly right, and your read is mostly right with it. Photo ratio, photo share of card, card gutter, section gap size, hero anchor ratio and a 17-value palette all sit inside or above the measured reference band. So 8 of the 13 defects are pure surface, 2 are content supply that no design change touches, and 3 genuinely need layout to move.

How to read this. Every number was taken with getBoundingClientRect and getComputedStyle on the rendered page at /de, never from source, and every one states its viewport: desktop is 1512 x 950, phone is 390 x 844. Left cell is ours, right cell is the floor we wrote or the reference band we measured. Floor break means a rule in our own system is broken. Taste gap means nothing in our system covers it, so the reference is the only standard. This page diagnoses. It proposes no layout and no treatment.

One number here disagrees with the research file behind it, and the page carries the newer one. Defect 4 was re-measured sub-pixel before publishing: the grouping ratio is 1.13x, not the 1.00x the probe reported, because the map-to-rail gap is 9.00px and not 8.00px. The floor is 2.00x, so the finding does not move. Twelve other headline numbers were re-measured the same way and every one reproduced exactly.

The thirteen, one by one

Each block: what it is in plain English, our number beside the floor or the reference, and one line on what the fix actually is.

1

A grey image placeholder is shipped on the live page

Content Floor break, three at once
Ours 38.9% of a 390 x 844 phone viewport is one grey square (358 x 358). At 1512 x 950 it is 576 x 576, or 23.1%. Live since 26 May 2026, which is 65 days.
Floor 0% dead grey zone. Finished-screen pass item (e), NEVER-AGAIN floor 4 (no muted focal), FLOORS LAW 2 (a photo-first surface never renders a bare grey box).

The business teaser holds a square of #F4F4F5 with a 56px picture-frame outline icon centred in it. That is the universal browser and operating-system signal for this image failed to load. It is not a neutral empty slot, it is an active claim that the page is broken, and it is the largest non-photographic element anywhere on the page. Its fill is 1.10:1 against the page white, so its own edge is barely perceivable and it reads as a hole rather than as a card.

Fix. Put a real partner-salon photograph in that square from data, or cut the square and let the text run full width. Restoring the old baked-in illustration is not available: a hardcoded hero image has been rejected three times and is gated. Same edit, two riders: the accessibility label carries a banned em-dash, and it misspells the brand as "Solon" to every screen reader that reaches it.

3

The salon card's five fields render as one grey block

Surface Floor break, LOCKFILE 17.4
Ours 1 anchor, at 500 Price is 12px / weight 400 / #6B6B6B, identical in all three properties to the postcode beside it. Three of five fields are pixel-identical, four of five are grey 400. The card's whole size range is 1.17x.
Floor, and the reference agrees 2 anchors, at 600 LOCKFILE 17.4: name (larger, 600) plus price (600, tabular). OpenTable's card is also 1.17x internally and Airtasker's is about 1.0x, so flat sizing is normal. Both buy the hierarchy back with a weight step plus a colour step on the deciding field. We buy nothing back.

On the card the customer actually decides from, the one thing the card exists to answer, what does this cost, carries no emphasis at all. This defect repeats 19 times per page, in the band that carries 29% of all text on the page.

Fix, and it is blocked before it is written. Two lines of code, but two live rules contradict each other and the card obeys the older one. LOCKFILE:479 (A13, 28 May) says the price recedes to grey 400. LOCKFILE:1921 (section 17.4, 7 June) says the price is a 600 tabular ink anchor, and calls itself the card-emphasis law. 17.4 is later, so it wins by our own precedence chain, but A13 is the one wired into the card primitives and into the drift checker, which would flag a 17.4-compliant card as drift forever. One sentence from you settles it.

4

Three relationships that mean three different things all render at 8 to 9px

Surface Floor break, FLOORS LAW 5
Ours 1.13x Between-group divided by in-group, at 390 x 844, sub-pixel exact. A photo to its own caption is 8.00. The map block to the rail below it is 9.00. A section heading to its own rail is 8.00. A heading to its own map is 5.00, which is 0.63x, so a heading is bound to its section less tightly than a photo is bound to its caption.
Floor, and the reference band 2.00x FLOORS LAW 5 requires at least 2x. Reference band for a heading to its own body: 14.3 to 20.9 pt across four measured screens. Three of our four sections sit below the bottom of that band.

The gap that says this text belongs to this photo is the same size as the gap that says these are two unrelated blocks. Nothing in the vertical spacing tells the eye what groups with what, so a column of real salons reads as a stack of loose parts.

It bites here specifically because the salon card carries no enclosure of its own: measured, its container is transparent, its border computes to 0px and it casts no elevation. The one depth cue is a 9%-alpha elevation under the photograph, and the text sits outside it. Proximity is therefore the only grouping signal the card has, and it is set to the value that means unrelated.

Fix. Four padding values in one file. Cost is about +56px of scroll at 390 on a 4316px document, or +1.3%. One line of later evidence on a settled call, not a reopened argument: the 2026-05-25 decision compressed this to chase a smaller between-section gap, and measured today the between-section gap is still 48 (correct by all four references) while the gap that actually shrank was the one holding a heading to its own content.

SectionHeader.tsx:378, :409, :474, :478 in app/[locale]/_components/homepage/

5

The whole content column is one unbroken white field

Surface Floor break, FLOORS LAW 4
Ours 97.3% white at 1512 x 950, and 95.5% at 390 x 844, sampled every 20px down the page. Three background bands exist and the only sunken one is in the footer. Photo-less sections are 62.6% of total section height on a phone.
Reference 8 of 10 measured reference bands give the band its own ground: a tinted band, a filled card, or a dark page. The 2 that keep white on white carry a hairline and nothing else.

Nothing on this page ever changes background. Two whole sections, the walk-in band and the reviews band, are rails of white cards on a white page with no photograph inside them and no tray underneath them. They have nothing to sit on, so they read as loose rows on a document.

Fix. Two sunken wrappers underneath the existing cards, which keep every bit of their chrome. This is the highest ratio of floors closed to lines changed on the whole list: it closes the missing-tray break and the no-alternation break in the same edit.

6

The loudest text in the feed is a wait estimate, in success green

Surface Taste gap on emphasis, semantic break, passes AA
Ours 20px / 700 / green The queue estimate is 1.67x its own body and is the only saturated colour anywhere on the page. Every salon name on the same screen is 14px grey-ink at weight 500. The green is unconditional, so a 98-minute wait renders in exactly the same success green as an available-right-now slot.
Reference Name or price Both reference apps reserve their loudest treatment for the entity name or the price, never for a derived estimate. Taste rule 4: success green is legal as an icon, never as body text.

Stated plainly so it is not over-read: this is not an accessibility break. Contrast was computed rather than quoted, 3.30:1, which at 20px / 700 qualifies as WCAG large text and passes AA. It is a meaning problem. The token that means good is spent on a slot rather than on a state, so it encodes nothing.

Fix. Either make the colour conditional on a real threshold, so the token starts encoding something, or take the figure to ink.

7

A phone user's first screen contains no photograph at all

Structural Floor break, FLOORS LAW 2
Ours 0.00% photographic area at 390 x 844. The six images in that viewport are all 49 x 49 category icons. The first photograph starts at document y=875, which is 31px below the fold. The screen is header 10.0%, hero 50.4%, gap 6.8%, category icon grid 29.7%.
Floor ~33% written at exactly 390 x 844, in FLOORS LAW 2 and in the design contract's imagery row. The page can clear it: its best photographic window is 40.59%, at y=700. It just does not clear it where the floor is written.

Ranked 7th and not 1st on purpose. It is a hard floor break, and it is nearly invisible to a person scrolling, because one flick reveals the photography. Ranked by our own rules it would sit near the top. Desktop is not a floor break, because no floor is written at a desktop width, but it is below every web reference at 8.38% (1512 x 950) and 11.76% (1280 x 800).

Fix, with the arithmetic so nobody guesses. One mobile card row contributes 390 x 185, or 21.9% of the viewport, so 33% needs roughly 1.5 photo bands in view, so the first photo band has to start by about y=500. That is a 375px pull-up. The zero-photo category grid is 251px of those 375.

8

Two typefaces render where one is specified

Surface Floor break, FLOORS LAW 8
Ours 30 elements compute a Helvetica Neue fallback at 1512 x 950, which is about one text element in ten, and the same 30 at 390 x 844. Same datum, two renders: a rating at 13px Inter in the card row, and the same rating at 12px Helvetica Neue in the map pin 90px below it.
Floor and reference 0 FLOORS LAW 8, the same thing looks the same everywhere. No reference screen in the whole corpus showed a system fallback face.

Cause confirmed on disk, not guessed: the map library's own stylesheet sets a font on the map container, and our marker element carries no font class, so it inherits. A system fallback font is the single most legible signal that a page is unfinished.

Fix. One class on one element. This is the cheapest real win on the entire list.

9

Ten font sizes in one viewport, and almost no range

Surface Floor break, the 4-size ceiling
Ours 10 sizes in the desktop first viewport (10, 12, 13, 13.5, 14, 15, 16, 20, 28, 44), and 15 across the whole page. 92.5% of all page text sits between 11 and 14px. In source: 42 distinct arbitrary pixel sizes, of which 267 occurrences are half-pixel steps.
Ceiling and references 4 Our own ceiling is 4 per screen. Measured first-viewport tier counts: Fresha 6, Airtasker 4, OpenTable 4.

A lot of sizes and almost no range. The extra tiers are 13.5 against 13, a 4% difference, which costs a size tier and buys no hierarchy at all.

Do not "fix" the ratios, they are fine and in places better than the references. Hero anchor 3.67x (Airtasker 3.2, OpenTable 2.8), section header to body 1.67x (OpenTable 1.66), card internal 1.17x (OpenTable 1.17). The count is the outlier, not the range.

Fix. A size scale in the Tailwind config plus a sweep. Deleting the half-steps alone takes the page from 15 rendered tiers to about 11 without changing how one pixel looks.

One salon renders as two different objects on one screen

Structural Floor break, FLOORS LAW 8 and imagery
Ours 2 anatomies for one entity, on one screen. The same salon is a 194 x 220 photo card, and 751px lower a 528 x 140 all-text row with a hairline, a green wait figure and no photograph. The walk-in band is 0% photographic at both viewports.

13

Four content insets on one page, and no section-rhythm token

Surface Consistency defect plus a design-contract break
Ours 4 insets, 2.17x spread Content starts at 140, 144, 146 and 148 from the left at 1512 x 950, a 2.8% spread. Section gaps spread 2.17x on desktop and 2.34x on a phone. 39.5% of rendered spacing sits off the 4pt scale the contract mandates. Phone gutters are a clean 16 everywhere.
Reference 1 inset, 1.00x to 1.12x References hold their section rhythm to within 1.12x down a single page, and start their content at one distance from the side.

The largest vertical separator declared anywhere in the five feed sections is 16px, so every real between-section gap, 44px to 124px, is an accidental sum of six separate declarations. Nobody chose any of them.

A 2.8% offset is the worst kind of inconsistency: too small to read as a decision, big enough to read as sloppiness. It is smaller than the 5% case our own grid research already named as the worst example it found, and therefore worse by that argument.

Fix. Align four padding values, and declare one section-gap token so the rhythm is chosen instead of summed.

Found while measuring

Surfaced, not edited. Each one currently hands out a wrong number to anyone who reads it.

  1. Two locked rules contradict each other on the card price. LOCKFILE:479 says it recedes to grey 400, LOCKFILE:1921 says it is a 600 ink anchor. The later one wins by our precedence chain, the older one is what is wired. This is the blocker on defect 3.
  2. LOCKFILE:625 publishes the wrong elevation value. It gives a two-layer recipe; both the Tailwind config and the live computed style say single-layer, with a dated reason in the config from 29 June. Anyone reading that table today gets the wrong number.
  3. The 86%-bold and 1.57x-anchor figures in the flatness diagnosis are stale. Both were re-measured, on the profile page and here. The rule stays useful as a ceiling; the numbers must not be re-quoted as current.
