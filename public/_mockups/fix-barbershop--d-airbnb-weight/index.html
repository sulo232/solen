Why this is the Airbnb mechanism and not a restyle

The owner named Airbnb as the source of truth on 2026-08-12. Measured that same month: Airbnb's own PDP runs about 3.1% of its text at weight 600 or heavier, seven elements out of 388, and carries its emphasis at weight 500 instead. This file does the same substitution here: nothing gets heavier than 500. Whatever used to stand out by being bold now has to stand out by being bigger or by colour, which is what the owner's own ladder says should carry hierarchy on every screen.

It is a real, working version of the fix, not a mockup of the idea: the change is a live script running against the real DOM, the same kind of script the other three variants of this page use to prove their own numbers.

What this does not touch. Weight and size are two different properties. Making text lighter does not make it smaller, move it, or change how much of the screen is a photo. The four other broken floors on this page, the 33% photo floor, the 28px anchor floor, the 1.8x size-ratio floor, and the 4-sizes-in-8px floor, are exactly where they were before. They are not this file's job and they do not move by accident here.

A real conflict this mechanism runs into, not smoothed over

Applied for real, sitewide, the way the owner described the ladder ("same ladder everywhere"), this exact change would also demote the SalonCard's name and price. Those two are locked to weight 600 by name in LOCKFILE.md (V3-D442, the two-anchor card rule), and a frozen lock outranks a taste call in this project's own precedence chain unless the owner reopens it by name.

Reopen V3-D442 for the card, sitewide Accept that the ladder wins: card name stays the visual anchor through SIZE alone (14px, larger than the 12px meta around it), price stays a plain weight-500 number next to it. This is what would make the after-panel above true everywhere the card renders, not just on this one page.
Keep the card's lock, demote everything else The card keeps its bold name and price exactly as V3-D442 locks it. Every other bold element on the page, the walk-in row's repeated names and status words, the section titles, still drops to 500. Weight share falls a lot, just not to the full 0% shown above, because the two locked card anchors stay bold on purpose.
An honest gap in the brief's numbers. The walk-in row shown in both panels above is live, real-time queue data, not a fixture. Loading this exact page five separate times during this task, the row's populated state (the one the 45.24% weight-share number describes) appeared in roughly half the loads within 15 seconds and did not appear in the other half. When it is empty or still loading, the page reads with far less bold text simply because there is less text on screen at all. The 45% number is real and reproduces reliably once the row has salons in it; it is not the number you would get opening this page at a random moment when the queue happens to be empty.
How this differs from the other three fixes being tried on this same page. One variant moves content up and enlarges it. One variant adds more real salon content. One changes only which weight each element is allowed to carry, this one. If this file's badges below show weight passing and the other four numbers unchanged, that is not a partial result, it is the whole, honest scope of what a weight-only change can do on this screen.
