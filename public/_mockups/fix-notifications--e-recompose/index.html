What you would actually see change

1. The title at the top gets bigger and calmer. The page heading grows from a small 20px bold line to a real 30px heading, but drops from bold to a lighter weight. Bigger now does the job bold used to do.

2. Bold text calms down everywhere else too. The mark-all-read control, the day headers, the unread row titles, all move to one lighter weight. The blue dot that already marks an unread row keeps doing that job on its own, it does not need the title bold as well.

3. Mark-all-read lines up with the row titles. It sat at its own odd size before, now it matches.

4. The list sits inside a light gray tray, the same one your salon page's service list already uses, instead of floating on plain white with only thin lines between rows. One soft shadow marks the outside of the tray, the thin lines between rows stay unchanged.

5. Rows tied to a real booking get a real photo. Any row that already connects somewhere real, a cancellation, a confirmed appointment, anything with a real booking behind it, swaps its generic icon for a small round photo, same size, same circle, nothing else moves. Rows with no booking or salon behind them (an application update, for instance) keep the plain icon, because there is nothing to show a photo of.

The one honest problem: a photo the size of an icon is still the size of an icon

Your phone screen is a fixed 844px tall. The imagery floor asks for a full third of that to be photographic. A notification row is 44px of icon, or now, 44px of photo, either way it is a small circle next to two lines of text, and that holds true of every messaging inbox in existence, not a Solen-specific miss.

What this change buys: in today's real seed data, exactly one 44px circle, on the one row that actually carries a real booking reference. That works out to roughly 0.6% of a 390×844 viewport. The floor is 33%. This treatment gets the inbox off zero, honestly, and does not pretend it cleared the floor. (A row further down names a salon in its own sentence but has no booking or salon reference stored on it, so it keeps its icon rather than getting a photo it cannot honestly earn; more rows would qualify once that data gap closes.)

Full compliance needs a bigger call than a row treatment, which is why it stays unapplied here without your go-ahead:

Two ways to actually close this, both his call, neither applied here:

  1. Name the inbox as exempt. The imagery floor already names four exempt surfaces by name: forms, checkout, legal, receipts, because they are system lists, not browsing surfaces. A notification inbox is arguably the same shape: you come here to clear it, not to browse it. If you agree, the fix is a one-line addition to that exemption list, and this floor stops being a problem to solve on this screen at all.
  2. Build a bigger, photo-led card format. Each notification becomes a taller card with a real leading photo, closer to how a booking confirmation reads elsewhere. That is a genuinely new component this registry does not own today, its own mockup, its own registry entry, and your go-ahead before anyone builds it.
