3. The path command, broken down

The entire heart is ONE <path d="..."/>. Six commands, traced in order:

d="M50 80 L20 50 a14 14 0 0 1 22 -18 l8 8 8 -8 a14 14 0 0 1 22 18 z"
   │     │     │                  │         │                  │
   1     2     3                  4         5                  6
1
M 50 80
Move to point P1 — the bottom tip of the heart. No line drawn yet, just position the pen.
2
L 20 50
Line to P2 — straight diagonal up-and-left to where the left curve will start.
3
a 14 14 0 0 1 22 -18
Arc (relative) — radius 14×14, sweep up-and-right by +22, -18 → forms the left "bump" of the heart, ending at P4 (50, 40), passing through P3 (42, 32).
4
l 8 8 8 -8
Two relative lines — down-right +8,+8 to bottom of V notch, then up-right +8,-8 to top of right bump start. The classic heart "dip" in the middle.
5
a 14 14 0 0 1 22 18
Arc (relative) — same radius 14×14, sweep down-and-right by +22, +18 → forms the right "bump", ending at P6 (80, 50).
6
z
Close path — auto-line straight from P6 back to P1, completing the heart.
Why one path? Drawing the whole heart as a single continuous path (instead of two arcs + a closing line as separate elements) lets the SVG renderer do the join math for the V notch and the bottom point. strokeLinejoin="round" softens those joins so the line-art reads as hand-drawn even though the geometry is precise.

4. The full SVG element

<svg
  width="80"
  height="80"
  viewBox="0 0 100 100"
  fill="none"
  stroke="#1B4D1B"           // brand green per Q21 + Q64
  strokeWidth="2"            // 2px line weight per Q22 illustration prompt
  strokeLinecap="round"      // soft endpoints — feels hand-drawn
  strokeLinejoin="round"     // soft corners at V notch and bottom point
  aria-hidden                // decorative — not announced by screen readers
>
  <path d="M50 80 L20 50 a14 14 0 0 1 22 -18 l8 8 8 -8 a14 14 0 0 1 22 18 z" />
</svg>

The 80×80 width/height is the rendered size; the 100×100 viewBox is the internal coordinate system. SVG scales the heart to fit. 14×14 pixel radius on the arcs means each "bump" is about a third of the heart's width — the proportion that reads as "heart" rather than "two bumps."
