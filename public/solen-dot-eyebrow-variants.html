Why C aligns with the evidence: Both Fresha + Uber omit eyebrows entirely on B2B section headers. The H2 itself IS the label. Eyebrows + dots are a 2018-2022 "marketing site template" trope (Webflow, Notion, Linear early-days). Both Fresha + Uber moved past it. Variant B is a middle ground that still reads as "template" because the uppercase tracked eyebrow alone signals that pattern.
What variant C costs: You lose the "scannability cue" that the eyebrow provided (a quick read of "category" before the heading). For a B2B marketing page with 6-8 sections, this is fine — the H2s themselves are scannable. For a long-form magazine article it might matter, but /fuer-salons isn't that.

Separator dots ≠ decoration dots

Worth distinguishing because they're different roles.

Separator dots (keep) — e.g. trust strip "1200+ Salons · Basel · Zürich · 4.9★": The dot is between facts — semantic role = "list separator." Uber + Fresha both use this in trust strips. Plain · middle-dot character or small 4px grey circle works.
Decoration dots (drop) — e.g. "• So funktioniert's" before a heading: No semantic role. Pure decoration. This is the template trope.

Recommend variant C — drop eyebrow + dot, sections go straight to H2

The evidence is uniform across both matched-surface peers. The pattern Solen has been shipping is the kind of "templated landing page" tell that makes a site feel AI-generated rather than human-designed. The fix is small (~15 min) and the registry rule (no eyebrow decoration) prevents future regressions via drift rule A12.

What changes: LOCKFILE §2.5 Eyebrow recipe gets an explicit "no leading dot, no leading icon" line. Drift rule A12 flags any rounded-full bg-s-* immediately before an eyebrow text node. /fuer-salons sweeps 2 callsites. SectionMeta primitive simplifies (drop dot, keep just uppercase label).
