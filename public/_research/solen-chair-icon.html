How a coiffeur and a barber read apart, my recommendation

The strongest evidence is in the reference itself: Airbnb's three icons are a house, a balloon and a bell. Three unrelated objects. They never tell two categories apart by changing one object slightly. So neither should we.

  1. Different chair, not a different person. Barber is the heavy pedestal chair, round chrome base, thick arms, footrest bar. Coiffeur is the backwash basin chair, reclined, with a white bowl behind the head on a slim five point base. The two differ at the top and at the bottom, which is exactly where the eye reads shape first.
  2. A colour per category. Barber keeps scarlet and chrome, which the world already reads as barbershop. Coiffeur takes blush and brass. At small sizes colour is recognised before shape, so this is the cue that survives when everything blurs.
  3. Hair length last. You were right that it will not carry this on its own. At 72 pixels it is a handful of pixels. It works as a third cue riding on top of the other two, never as the first.

A side benefit worth naming: because the figure stays a smooth featureless clay person in both, we never cast a face, a skin tone or a gender into a permanent brand mark. The chair says which service it is. The person is just a person.

Safari, solved

Safari cannot play a transparent WebM, and Apple's own answer, HEVC with an alpha channel, refuses to encode on this Mac: the hardware encoder returns error 12908 across four different flag combinations. Animated WebP would have been ideal at about 53 KB, but this machine has no libwebp encoder at all.

So every icon now also ships as an APNG, which carries real transparency, keeps the frame timing, plays once and stops on its last frame, and works in Safari, iOS, Chrome and Firefox alike. The images below are the APNGs, not the videos.

Still honest about what is not right

  • The coiffeur is still the brightest thing here at 0.72, because pink, cream and white all sit high to begin with. Pulling it down further starts greying the pink, so it stays until you say otherwise.
  • Colour balance is the real remaining problem and it is measurable. Airbnb's set is mostly neutral: their house is 65 percent neutral pixels and their bell 64 percent, with only the balloon loud at 2 percent. Ours is 14 percent neutral, so nearly the whole object is shouting. Ours also mixes red and blue, two opposite families, while every Airbnb icon stays inside one family. That is the fix for the next pass, not more brightness.
  • No Safari file for either. This Mac's encoder refuses transparent HEVC, so today they play in Chrome and Firefox only.
  • Each mesh is reconstructed from one picture, so the back of each chair is guessed and reads softer than the front. Feeding three views fixes it.
  • The coiffeur basin is a first attempt at the idea, not a settled design. If you like the direction, the next pass sharpens it.
  • Neither is wired into any Solen screen. That is a separate step and it goes through mockup approval first.
