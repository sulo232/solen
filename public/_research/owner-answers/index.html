1. "ab CHF" is not allowed in Switzerland

You asked: "2 research whats legal"

This is not a grey area and not a matter of interpretation. SECO writes it out for exactly your industry, in the information sheet for hairdressers, barbershops, cosmetic institutes and body care dated 1 April 2025:

"The prices shown are fixed prices. Entries such as «beard trim: from Fr. 30.» are not permitted." The binding original, SECO Merkblatt 01.04.2025: "Die angegebenen Preise sind fixe Preise. Angaben wie «Bart schneiden: ab Fr. 30.-» sind nicht zulaessig."

The reason sits in Art. 10 para. 1 of the price-disclosure ordinance: for these services the price the customer actually pays must be shown with the offer. Hairdressing is letter a, cosmetics and body care is letter d. Your entire catalogue falls under it.

When a price genuinely varies, the legal way to say so is not "from", it is tiers. SECO gives the example itself:

Blow-dry

Medium hairCHF 50
Long hairCHF 55

Two more points that hit you directly. The price has to be on the offer, not only at checkout , that sentence was put into the law in 2022 specifically to overturn a Federal Supreme Court ruling that had said the opposite. And any non-optional fee (reservation, service, processing) must be inside the price you display. That one matters if you ever introduce a booking fee.

Now fixed on the cards too

The salon card showed a from-price, the cheapest service in that whole salon, without saying which service it was. That is the one condition SECO attaches to a from-price in advertising, so the card now names the service.

2. You already have the good clause

You asked: "cant we incl in the terms n service all pictures uploaded are mine"

I read the actual terms of Airbnb, Fresha, Uber, Booking.com, Treatwell, Yelp and Google. None of the six takes ownership. All six take a licence, and Uber and Google go out of their way to state that the content stays the user's.

And your terms already do this , the salon keeps ownership, Solen gets a worldwide, royalty-free licence covering promotional use. That is exactly the industry standard. Under Swiss copyright law "all pictures are mine" would also be partly unenforceable anyway: moral rights cannot be assigned at all.

What is actually missing is something else. A salon photo is usually a photo of a client , and she never agreed to anything. Fresha and Treatwell both require the salon to get her consent before uploading, keep a record of it, and delete the photo if she withdraws it. Your terms say nothing about that. That is the real gap.

Your decision, the only one left here

Not the checkbox , that part is trivial. The question is what you promise when someone in a photo objects. For example "we remove it within 48 hours, no questions asked". That is policy, not code, and it sets your support load.

3. There is no plan for the day something breaks

You answered: "9 yr we need whole principle for that" , now written

The principle in one sentence: stop the bleeding before you understand it. And write down what is happening while it happens, not afterwards.

The 9pm ladder , the first three steps need no diagnosis

  1. Write the clock. The time, what you saw, and how you found out.
  2. How bad? Money or data wrong = act now. Core flow dead with no workaround = urgent. Unsure between two levels? Always the higher one, and never debate it during the incident.
  3. Stop it. Shipped something in the last hour? Netlify, last good build, publish. Otherwise pull the feature flag , live within 30 seconds.

Three things in there are specific to you, and are otherwise learned the expensive way:

  • The kill switch fails open , if Supabase is down, flipping it does nothing.
  • maintenance_mode stops writes, not pages.
  • Never disable the Stripe webhook. Anything that arrives while it is off is gone forever. An error instead gets retried for three days.

The uncomfortable part: nothing wakes you. No Sentry, no SMS, no push. The fastest automatic signal is a check every 15 minutes that sends an email. Everything else waits for the 05:15 summary. A problem that starts at 6pm Friday and does not take the whole site down is invisible to you until Saturday morning.

Your decision

Should anything call you? The cheapest option is a free push channel (ntfy or Pushover) wired next to the alert email you already have, limited to the worst tier. Costs nothing but an afternoon. It also means your phone can wake you, which may be exactly what you don't want.
