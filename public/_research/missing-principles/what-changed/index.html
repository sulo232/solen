The eight things waiting on you

Each is a judgement call, not a task. I stopped on purpose. Ordered by what it costs to leave undecided.

yours1. Nobody has photographed your salons

Your cards now show a photo, which is the right call. But there are no real photos. The gallery table is empty for all 28 salons and the covers are stock pictures from Unsplash. Four different salons currently show the same photograph. So the page looks right and is telling a small lie about every business on it.

Why yoursNo amount of code produces a photograph of a real hairdresser. Someone shoots them, or asks the salons for their own.
If you waitYou launch showing strangers' interiors as if they were your partners' shops. A salon owner notices that immediately.
I did doAdded a check that reports this every time the invariants run, so it cannot quietly persist.
yours2. Your rulebook and your code disagree about the empty-photo card

When a salon has no photo, your written law says the card shows a soft grey background, a category icon and the salon's first letter. Two card components ship two different other things: one paints a solid colour block with the whole name in giant type, the other a grey block with only a letter and no icon. Three descriptions, two implementations, none matching.

Why yoursIt changes how a customer-facing card looks, and you told me to ask before visible design changes.
The choiceMake the code match the rule, or pick one shipped version and rewrite the rule to match it. Either is fine. Three is not.
yours3. The French site is formal, the German and Italian ones are not

Your design notes say the warm informal register was chosen on purpose, because formality reads as distance. German follows it (196 informal words against 12 formal), Italian almost perfectly (245 against 1). French does the opposite: 229 formal against 46 informal. Not only in legal text, in the home page and the marketing copy.

Why yoursBrand voice. Changing it touches roughly 5,600 strings, and French-speaking Switzerland does reach for "vous" more readily than German-speaking Switzerland reaches for "Sie".
The choiceKeep French formal deliberately and write that down as an exception, or bring it in line with the other three.
yours4. Anyone can publish a photo to your public site with nobody looking

A salon owner or reviewer uploads an image and it goes straight to a public web address. No queue, no approval, no hidden-until-checked state. Your Discovery content does get reviewed. These two paths do not.

Why yoursIt is a product decision with a real cost: somebody has to look at the queue. That is your time or a moderator's.
If you waitHarmless today with no real users. On day one it is one bad upload away from a problem you handle in public.
yours5. Nobody confirms they are allowed to upload the photo

A review photo can show a stylist's face, or another customer in the background. A gallery photo could be lifted from a competitor. The upload asks nothing and records nothing about rights or consent.

Why yoursAdding a checkbox is easy. Deciding what it says, and what you promise when someone objects, is policy.
yours6. "ab CHF 35" on a service that has only one price

"ab" means "from". On a salon it is plainly right: prices start at 35. On a single service with one fixed price, "from 35" arguably implies something cheaper exists. Swiss price-display rules care about that distinction.

Why yoursA legal interpretation, and the finding's own confidence was "assume". I do not rewrite money-facing copy on a guess, in either direction.
I did doWrote the full analysis into _rules/LEGAL_COPY.md so whoever answers it has the picture.
yours7. A backup has never actually been restored

Backups are being taken. The runbook states recovery in one to two hours. That number has never been tested; the existing script proves the export works, not the restore. An untested backup is a hope.

Why yoursProving a restore means writing real data into a throwaway database. No agent of mine performs a database write without you saying so.
The askOne authorisation and about an hour. Then the runbook number is a measurement instead of a guess.
yours8. The dashboard stretches edge to edge on a big monitor

Nothing constrains the dashboard's width, so on a 27-inch screen every row, field and tile runs the full window and lines of text get very long to read. The customer site caps its line length; the dashboard never learned to.

Why yoursIt changes how a screen looks, so it is your call. It is also about five minutes of work once you say yes.

What is actually different now

Grouped by what it protects. Every number re-checked against the repo this turn rather than copied from a report.

safetyThings that could have hurt someone
  • A salon owner could have run code on your site through their own salon name. The page put that name inside a script block without escaping it. One shared helper now escapes every database value that reaches a script tag, with a Content-Security-Policy header behind it as a second net.
  • Uploads were trusted on the client's word. Nine upload routes now read the file's actual first bytes instead of believing the label, strip GPS and camera metadata (a customer photo can carry the coordinates of someone's home), and require a header a forged cross-site form cannot send.
  • A migration would have invented wheelchair access. A seed file set nine amenity flags, wheelchair accessible among them, from a hash of the salon id. Not live, but any restore would have made it live. Now inert, with the reason recorded.
honestyThings the product claimed but did not do
  • Your Terms promise phone verification. The check said "verified" and wrote nothing, because there is no column to write to. It no longer claims a success it cannot record, and the missing piece is named where the next person will find it.
  • Your Terms promise strikes and suspensions. The table was written to in three places and read by nobody, so the consequence could never fire. It has a reader now.
  • A gate your rulebook said was protecting you had never been switched on. Seven were in that state. All seven are armed, and a new check fails whenever a rule claims a protection that is not running.
four languagesThings that only really worked in German
  • Every page told browsers and screen readers it was German, whatever it was showing. Now it says the right one, and the skip link is translated.
  • Dates and prices formatted as Swiss German everywhere. Over a hundred places now follow the reader's language through one shared helper instead of copies, with a lint rule so the next one cannot creep in.
  • A refund screen was English-only in German, French and Italian, with a to-translate marker sitting in the file for weeks. Translated, and a parity check now fails if a language falls behind.
silenceThings that failed without telling anyone
  • A scheduled job that never ran read as zero failures. The daily check only counted jobs that ran and failed. It now alerts on absence, which is the shape most of these failures actually take.
  • Payment webhook rejections only wrote to a log. They now reach the same alert path as everything else.
honest caveatWhat is not fixed
  • 58 findings are still unverified either way. No commit names them. Some are genuinely unshipped, including ten rule-system items an agent reported done that left no trace on disk. I would rather show them open than claim them.
  • Your test and lint CI still has never run. Only one workflow file exists on GitHub. That one needs you.
