Reference capture, 31 July 2026

Airbnb's animated icons, measured frame by frame

Nine real animation files pulled off Airbnb's CDN, decoded to 411 individual frames, and measured. No guessing from memory: every number below came out of the actual assets or out of the live page.

Source: a0.muscache.com/videos/search-bar-icons/, linked from the search bar on airbnb.com (desktop web, 1440px). Assets and frames live in public/_pixel-refs/airbnb/icons-motion/.

1. The real thing. Click any icon to replay it.

These are Airbnb's own files, served locally, playing at their native 30fps with their real transparent background. Nothing here is a recreation.

Each file plays once and stops on its last frame. That last frame is the icon you see at rest on airbnb.com.

2. What Airbnb actually ships (and what it is not)

The internet says Airbnb invented a proprietary 3D icon format called "Lava". On the live web product today, that is not what is being served. The search-bar icons are ordinary video files with an alpha channel.

3. Three clips per icon, not one

This is the part that is easy to miss. Each icon is not one animation. It is a set of three, and the set is what makes the interaction feel alive.

ClipLengthWhat it is and when it runs
house-twirl1.700 sThe inactive icon does one full turn. Fires once on page load. Parks on its final frame, which becomes the resting icon.
house-twirl-selected1.700 sThe same full turn, ending in the active pose. This is what the currently active tab plays on load.
house-selected0.767 sA short settle. Fires when you click the tab. The object turns a few degrees toward you and stops. No spin.

Every twirl in the set is exactly 1.700 s and 51 frames. The short clips are not equal length: house 0.767 s, balloon 1.533 s, concierge bell 1.033 s. The length follows the object, not a token.

5. The timing inside one clip

The clip is 1.700 s but the movement is not. Airbnb bakes a still hold into both ends of the file, so the animation has a beat before it starts and comes fully to rest before the file ends.

ClipFramesLengthStill, startMovingStill, endPeak movement

Movement is measured as per-frame pixel change inside the alpha mask, so it is not fooled by an object that spins without changing silhouette. The balloon and the bell both do exactly that.

6. The choreography on the page

TriggerWhat happens
Page loadAll three icons twirl once, staggered left to right. Measured gaps between the three play() calls across two independent sessions: 152 and 148 ms, then 158 and 142 ms. So the stagger is about 150 ms.
Hover a tabNothing. No play, no seek, no transform. Measured by hooking HTMLMediaElement.play and currentTime before page load, then hovering each tab for 1.4 s.
Click a tabThe short clip plays once from 0. The twirl videos are dropped from the DOM and the short clips are faded in by opacity.
At restTwo <video> elements sit stacked at the same coordinates, one at opacity: 1 and one at 0, crossfaded by a CSS transition. The visible one is paused on its final frame.
IdleNo loop, no ambient wiggle, no replay on scroll. It plays once and is then a still picture until you act.

7. The easing, and why it is not what you would guess

Normal UI motion is fast out, slow in. These icons are not that. I tracked the moving centroid of the short clip across all 23 frames and fitted it against the standard curves:

CurveFit error, lower is closer
linear0.040
ease-in-out (.42,0,.58,1)0.066
ease-out (0,0,.58,1)0.127
material standard (.4,0,.2,1)0.162
ease-out-quart (.25,1,.5,1)0.324

Nearly linear, with soft ends. That is the signature of an object with mass turning, not of a UI element snapping into place. It is why these read as physical rather than as interface.

8. The philosophy, in six lines

  • The icon is a small physical object, filmed. It is not a diagram that got a transition.
  • Motion is a greeting, not a state. It plays once on arrival and then holds perfectly still.
  • The rest frame is the last frame of the animation, so the movement never contradicts the icon.
  • The turn is near linear with soft ends, because things with mass do not ease out.
  • Clicking gets a smaller, shorter move than arriving does. Choosing is a nod, not a performance.
  • Clip length follows the object, not a global duration token. The balloon takes twice as long as the house.

9. What I did not measure

  • The exact rotation angle per frame. The clips show a full turn, but I did not solve the camera, so any claim like "it rotates 90 degrees by frame 12" would be invented.
  • The iOS and Android app icons. This capture is desktop web only. The apps may well ship the Lava format those flags refer to.
  • The mobile web variant at 390px.
  • How the icons were authored (Blender, Cinema 4D, something else). Not knowable from the output.
  • The icon set sits behind an A/B test: 1 in 2 fresh sessions rendered a plain text tab bar with no icons at all. Everything above is the variant that has them.

10. What this means for a Solen barber-chair icon

Read as a recipe, this is buildable without any proprietary format. This section is a proposal to react to, not something that exists in Solen yet:

  • Author in 3D, render a turn at 30fps on a transparent background, export a frame sequence.
  • Encode VP9 WebM with alpha, plus an HEVC .mov with alpha for Safari. Both at roughly 2.5x the display size.
  • Keep the clip under about 1.7 s and under about 120 KB.
  • Bake a still hold onto both ends of the clip, and make the final frame the resting icon.
  • Play once on mount, staggered about 150 ms when there is more than one. Never loop. Never play on hover.
  • Ship a second, shorter clip for the chosen state.

One open question before anything gets built: a person sitting in a rotating salon chair is a different subject from a house or a balloon. A human figure raises a casting decision (skin tone, hair, gender read) that a house does not, and that call is yours, not mine.

Captured with a headless Chromium session against the live site, decoded with ffmpeg (libvpx-vp9, RGBA), measured with PIL and NumPy. Every page load was guarded against error status, off-host redirect, error UI at status 200, and an unrendered body, so nothing here was measured off a broken page.
