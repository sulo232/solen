## Stripe Dashboard

**Chart treatment.** Line chart, no bars on the home overview (bars appear only in Billing/Growth sub-pages for discrete monthly buckets like MRR growth). Line hue is a saturated indigo/purple, estimated `#635BFF` to `#6772E5` (Stripe's brand purple), the same hue Stripe uses everywhere else in the product, not a separate "chart color." On the "Today" gross-volume chart the line is a lighter blue-violet, closer to `#7A73FF`. No gradient area-fill under the primary line on the compact overview tiles (flat single-px line only), but the larger "Today" hero chart shows a very faint tint near the axis, not a strong gradient. Gridlines: none visible, axis has only start/end date labels and a $ min/max, no horizontal rule lines. Endpoint dot: yes, a single filled circle marks the current/last data point on the line (visible on "Gross volume," "Net volume from sales," "New customers" cards). Hover reveals a tooltip with a vertical guide line and a filled dot at the hovered point (seen in the MRR growth chart and Payment methods report).

**Stat/KPI presentation.** The number is large and bold, sitting directly under a small plain-weight label (e.g. label "Gross volume" ~13px regular gray, number "$41.54" ~24-28px bold ink black). Ratio of number-to-label size is roughly 2:1. Delta styling: a small pill badge next to the label, pale green background with a plus/up green text for positive (e.g. "+0.0%" style badges), pale red/pink for negative deltas (seen as "-38.6%" in red text without a pill on one capture, and as a green pill "+0.0%" on another, so Stripe mixes plain colored text and pale pill badges for deltas depending on the surface, the home "Today" summary uses plain text, the "Your overview" card grid uses pill badges). No arrows observed, color and sign carry the direction, not an icon.

**Color model.** Neutral base dominates almost entirely: white cards, light gray page background (`#F7F8FA`-ish), black/dark-gray ink text. The ONE saturated hue is Stripe's indigo-purple, used consistently for every line chart, every primary button ("+Add," "Done," "Create"), and small UI accents (toggle "Test mode" switch, selected nav-item underline). Semantic colors are separate and distinct from the brand hue: green for succeeded/positive, red for failed/negative, orange/amber for the "Test mode" and warning banners, these never borrow the purple. Overall saturated-color coverage is low, maybe 3-5% of any given screen (a button, a line, a badge), everything else is white/gray/black. This is a textbook restrained palette.

**Nav/chrome.** Top horizontal nav bar (not a sidebar on the main Home dashboard), items: Home, Payments, Balances, Customers, Products, Billing, Reports, Connect, More. Active item state: the active tab gets a colored underline in the brand purple plus the text itself rendered in that same purple/indigo (seen on "Home," "Billing" both underlined and colored when active), inactive items are plain dark gray text, no fill/pill background on the active state. On the "Content" test-mode screen (the card-heavy customizable Home), there IS a left sidebar instead of top nav, with items like Home, Balances, Transactions, Customers, Product catalogue, and nested Products/Billing/Reporting groups, active item there gets a light purple-tinted rounded background (a sunken pill fill) plus purple text, not just an underline. So Stripe actually runs two chrome patterns depending on account type/view, both keep the same restraint: color only on the active item, never a saturated block nav.

**Cards.** Radius roughly 8-10px (Stripe's characteristic small-medium radius, tighter than most modern SaaS). Border-based, not shadow-based, thin 1px light gray hairline border around each card, no drop shadow visible in any capture. Padding density is moderate-to-tight, roughly 16-20px inside each stat tile, cards sit in a tight grid with small gutters (~12-16px) between them.

**Type.** Sans-serif system-adjacent face (Stripe uses its own "Camphor"/"Söhne"-style grotesk, reads similar to a clean neutral grotesk in the capture, not a geometric font). Hero numbers ~24-28px bold. Section headers ("Your overview," "Today," "Growth") ~20-24px bold black. Card labels ~13px regular gray. Table/list rows ~13-14px regular.

## Fresha (partner web dashboard)

**Chart treatment.** Dual-line chart mixing two series on the "Recent sales" home card: a purple line ("Sales") and a green line ("Appointments"), both saturated, estimated purple `#7C3AED`/`#8B5CF6` and green `#22C55E`/`#16A34A`. Small filled dot markers at every data point along both lines (not just the endpoint), a denser dotted look than Stripe's single-endpoint-dot style. On the "Upcoming appointments" card, a bar chart appears instead, dual-color stacked/grouped bars: purple for "Confirmed" and red/pink for "Cancelled." Gridlines: faint horizontal gridlines are visible behind the bar chart (light gray, very subtle), the line chart has none. No gradient area-fill under either line, both are flat strokes. Hover state (seen mid-interaction) shows a vertical dotted guide line plus a small dark tooltip box listing both series values for that date.

**Stat/KPI presentation.** Very large bold number directly under a small label, e.g. label "Recent sales" (~13px gray) then "$2,085.00" at roughly 28-32px bold black, a bigger ratio than Stripe's (~2.5:1). Below the headline number, a secondary row of smaller supporting stats in plain gray-black text ("Appointments 31," "Appointments value $1,847.50") at ~13px, no color coding, no delta/percentage badge visible on the home card (Fresha's home overview omits period-over-period deltas entirely on this view, unlike Stripe and Shopify).

**Color model.** Fresha's own brand identity is a deep purple/near-black used for the sidebar and the "Complete Setup" pill in the header, but the working canvas (calendar, cards) is almost entirely white/light-gray with black ink text. The saturated colors that DO appear are functional/semantic, not brand-decorative: purple for "sales" series and "confirmed" bookings, green for "appointments" count and completed status, red/coral for "cancelled" and blocked/red calendar events, blue-gray for booked/pending calendar blocks. Coverage of saturated color is slightly higher than Stripe, maybe 8-12% of the screen, mostly concentrated in the calendar grid (colored appointment blocks) and the two home charts.

**Nav/chrome.** Full-height LEFT sidebar, solid near-black/very dark charcoal background (estimated `#0F0F10` to `#18181B`), icon-only (no text labels visible in the captured width), roughly 8-9 icon items stacked vertically (home, calendar, tag/services, smiley/marketing, book, contacts, megaphone, briefcase/payments, chart, grid, gear). Active item state: the active icon gets a small rounded-square white/light background chip behind the icon (a sunken pill fill, similar mechanism to Stripe's purple-tint active state but neutral white-on-black here instead of colored), sitting inside the otherwise all-black rail, clearly legible against the dark chrome.

**Cards.** Radius roughly 12-16px, distinctly rounder than Stripe's 8-10px. Border-based (thin light gray hairline), no visible drop shadow on resting cards. Padding is generous, roughly 20-24px, cards feel airier/less dense than Stripe's tighter grid.

**Type.** Clean grotesk sans, slightly rounder letterforms than Stripe's face, reads like a standard modern UI grotesk (Inter/Söhne-adjacent). Hero numbers ~28-32px bold. Card titles ~14-15px semibold. Calendar event text is small, ~11-12px, tight line-height to fit inside short time-slot blocks.

## Shopify Admin

**Chart treatment.** Line chart exclusively across every capture (Home, Analytics, Finances, Live View), no bar charts observed on any dashboard surface. Line hue is Shopify's signature bright green, estimated `#008060` to `#00A47C` in most captures, though a couple of "Total sales"/"Finances" tiles render the line in a soft blue instead (estimated `#5C6AC4`-ish), so Shopify is not perfectly consistent, green dominates but isn't exclusive. Gradient area-fill: YES, consistently, a soft tinted fill under the line fading from the line's hue at low opacity down to transparent at the baseline, visible on nearly every sparkline (Total sales, Online store sessions, Total orders, Average order value, Returning customer rate). This is Shopify's most distinctive chart signature versus the other three products. Gridlines: none inside the small dashboard sparklines, the larger Analytics page charts show faint dollar-axis labels only, no horizontal rules. Endpoint dot: yes, a single circle at the last point, plus on hover a dotted vertical guide with a dark tooltip box (seen on the Analytics comparison chart with two date ranges overlaid, solid line for current period, dashed/dotted line for the comparison period).

**Stat/KPI presentation.** Bold large number under a small plain label, e.g. "Total sales" (~12-13px gray) then "$18.90" at ~20-24px bold black, similar ratio to Stripe (~2:1), noticeably smaller absolute size than Fresha's hero number. A small delta indicator sits inline next to the label or number as a plain flat-line dash glyph when flat/zero, or a colored percentage with a down/up arrow when there's real change (seen as "↓50%" in red next to "Sessions," red text plus a down-caret glyph, not a pill badge). So Shopify's delta styling is plain colored text + arrow glyph, not a pill, distinct from Stripe's pill-badge pattern.

**Color model.** Base is white cards on a very light warm-gray page background, black ink text. The saturated brand green is reserved for charts, links, and primary buttons/active nav only. Semantic red appears only for negative deltas and alert banners. Overall saturated-color presence is low, similar restraint level to Stripe, maybe 4-6% of screen. Shopify's "Winter '23" older UI captures show a slightly warmer gray chrome than the newer captures (plain white sidebar, black wordmark logo), while newer captures show a cleaner white-on-white with a green circular avatar badge, small UI refinements across versions but the same restraint principle throughout.

**Nav/chrome.** Left sidebar, white background (not dark, unlike Fresha), text+icon rows (Home, Orders, Products, Customers, Content, Finances, Analytics, Marketing, Discounts), with a nested "Sales channels" and "Apps" section below. Active item state: a light gray rounded rectangle fill behind the active row (a sunken pill, same mechanism family as Stripe's purple-tint and Fresha's white-chip, here rendered neutral gray) plus the label switches to bold/darker text, no color change, no left bar. This is the most "neutral selected state" of all four products, pure gray fill, no hue at all.

**Cards.** Radius roughly 8px, tight and squared-ish similar to Stripe, on the low end of the four products. Thin 1px light gray border, no shadow observed on resting cards in any capture. Padding moderate, roughly 16-20px, laid out in a dense 3-column grid on Analytics/Live View pages.

**Type.** Shopify's own grotesk (reads like a slightly more geometric/rounded sans than Stripe's, consistent with Shopify's actual "Inter"-adjacent product typeface). Hero numbers ~20-24px bold. Section header "Analytics"/"Finances" ~20-22px bold. Sidebar nav labels ~13-14px regular/medium.

## CONVERGENCE

Eight treatment rules all four real products share, read across every capture above:

1. **Line charts on the primary revenue/sales metric, always.** Every product defaults its top-of-dashboard money metric to a line, not a bar or a gauge. Bars only appear for secondary/comparison views (bookings confirmed vs cancelled, monthly buckets, team-member breakdowns). If Solen's dashboard leads with one hero revenue chart, line is the converged choice, not bar.

2. **One brand hue does double duty as the chart line AND the primary button/active-nav color, and nothing else gets that hue.** Stripe purple, Fresha purple, Shopify green, Square blue, in each case the exact same saturated color appears on the chart line, the primary CTA button, and the active nav indicator, and essentially nowhere else structural. Estimated hexes: Stripe `#635BFF`, Fresha `#7C3AED`/`#8B5CF6`, Shopify `#008060`, Square `#006AFF`-ish.

3. **Saturated color coverage is low, roughly 3-12% of any given screen.** Stripe and Shopify sit at the low end (~4-6%), Fresha a bit higher (~8-12% via its colored calendar blocks), Square lowest structurally but with one loud exception (see rule 8). None of the four fill large card backgrounds or nav rails with saturated brand color, the base is always 85%+ white/light-gray/black-ink.

4. **The number is always bold and roughly 1.5x to 2.5x the label's size, and the label is always plain-weight gray, directly above or below the number, never beside it.** This label-then-number vertical stack, quiet label / loud number, is identical across all four despite different absolute sizes (Stripe ~24-28px, Fresha ~28-32px, Shopify ~20-24px, Square ~18-20px).

5. **Active nav state is a fill or color change on the item itself, never a separate colored bar or icon-only indicator.** Four different mechanisms, same principle: Stripe = purple-tint pill background + purple text (sidebar variant) or underline (top-nav variant), Fresha = white chip on black rail, Shopify = gray pill fill, Square = plain blue text swap with no fill at all. All four skip the "colored left-edge bar" pattern entirely, which matches this project's existing dashboard lock against a left-edge bar.

6. **Cards are border-first, not shadow-first, at rest.** All four use a thin 1px light-gray hairline border around resting cards with zero or near-zero drop shadow. Elevation/shadow, where it appears at all (tooltips, hover popovers), is reserved for content that floats above the base grid, never for a normal resting stat tile.

7. **Endpoint dots and hover tooltips are universal, gridlines are optional and product-specific.** Every product marks the latest data point with a filled dot and reveals a tooltip + vertical guide line on hover. But full horizontal gridlines are inconsistent, Square uses them most (bar-heavy reports), Stripe and Fresha mostly omit them on line sparklines, Shopify omits them entirely. So dot+tooltip is a converged "must have," gridlines are a converged "optional, product taste."

8. **Radius is small-to-moderate and product-specific, never sharp and never a large 20px+ storytelling radius.** Square ~4-8px (squarest), Stripe ~8-10px, Shopify ~8px, Fresha ~12-16px (roundest of the four). None exceed roughly 16px on a structural dashboard card, this is meaningfully tighter than the 20-28px "hero card" range the prior owner-screenshot spec (`modern-dashboard-spec-2026-07-06.md`) found in non-dashboard marketing/popover references. Read together: dashboards specifically converge tighter than general "modern SaaS" UI.

**Where they differ (do not average these away):**
- **Gradient area-fill under the line**: Shopify does it on nearly every sparkline (its single most distinctive signature); Stripe, Fresha, and Square do not use it at all. This is a real product-identity choice, not a universal rule.
- **Delta styling**: Stripe mixes plain colored text and pale pill badges depending on surface; Shopify uses plain colored text + arrow glyph, no pill; Fresha shows no delta at all on its home cards; Square shows a caret+percentage with color not clearly asserted from the zero-state sample captured.
- **Chrome model**: Stripe runs top-nav on its main Home and left-sidebar on its customizable/Content view (two patterns in the same product); Fresha and Shopify and Square are all consistently left-sidebar only.
- **Label case**: Square alone uses tracked small-caps labels ("GROSS SALES"); the other three use normal-case gray labels. This is a Square-specific typographic signature, not shared.
- **Willingness to go full-saturation-block**: Square's Loyalty/onboarding celebratory banners go full solid green, a real exception to rule 3's low-coverage convergence; the other three never render a large solid-color block anywhere in the captured surfaces.
