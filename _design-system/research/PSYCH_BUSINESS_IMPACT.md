# Business Impact of UX: Solen.ch Research Synthesis

## Top principles (ranked)

1. **Guest checkout beats forced account creation**: Baymard's checkout research (200,000+ hours, 20,240+ participants) puts forced account creation among the top cited abandonment causes; separately, 19-24% of US shoppers report abandoning a cart specifically because of it. Spool's UIE case study corroborates the mechanism with a real before/after (~$6M week-one lift, ~80% fewer password resets), though the "$300M/year" figure is an unverifiable projection. **Solen application**: let a user pick service/staff/time without an account; ask only for phone/email at the pay step, with a guest option next to login. **Smallest lever**: remove the account gate before payment; keep it optional after confirmation. **Confidence: high.**

2. **Unexpected costs revealed late kill more bookings than flow length itself**: Baymard's abandonment breakdown shows 39% of US adults abandoned a purchase due to hidden/late fees versus 18% for "too long". **Solen application**: platform fees or surcharges must show at services/staff selection, not first at the pay screen. **Smallest lever**: move the total-with-fees display one step earlier. **Confidence: high.**

3. **Deposit/prepayment and pay-upfront reduce no-shows via sunk-cost commitment, but exact percentages are unverified vendor claims**: The direction (prepay reduces no-shows) has some real backing (a German study found 1.8% no-show for online-booked vs 5.9% offline, PMC, n=16,894) and behavioral-economics theory, but the specific "40-90% reduction" numbers circulating are vendor blogs with no traceable methodology. **Solen application**: Solen's walk-in pay-upfront model is already structurally sound; extending prepay to standard bookings should be scoped to highest-risk segments (new customers, high-value services) and A/B tested on Solen's own data, not sold internally on borrowed percentages. **Confidence: medium (mechanism) / low (magnitude).**

4. **SMS reminders reliably cut no-shows, and a second reminder adds further reduction**: Multiple RCTs converge: a 161,587-appointment RCT across 14 hospitals cut no-shows from 21.1% to 14.2% with consequence-framed SMS (OR 0.69, p<0.001); a Kaiser Permanente pragmatic RCT (125,076 + 33,593 visits) found a second reminder cut no-shows a further 7-11% relatively; a pediatric RCT (n=169-170) found text-plus-voice beat voice alone (23.5% vs 38.1%). This is the best-replicated finding in the whole dataset. **Solen application**: send SMS (not just push) reminders at both 24h and a closer touchpoint (2h), with copy naming the concrete cost of missing the slot. **Smallest lever**: turn on a second reminder and switch default channel from push-only to SMS. **Confidence: high.**

5. **Review count matters as much as star average, and even a handful of reviews produces most of the trust lift**: Northwestern Spiegel Research Center: purchase likelihood is 270% higher with 5 reviews than 0; PowerReviews' 20M-page analysis shows conversion climbs steeply from 3.0 to 4.0 stars. Both are large observational datasets (correlational, not causal-experiment), consistent across sources. **Solen application**: never hide low review counts, always show count next to the star, and make requesting a review from a salon's first bookings a tracked onboarding milestone. **Smallest lever**: surface review count on every card and PDP, no threshold-based suppression. **Confidence: medium-high.**

6. **A near-perfect 5.0 rating converts worse than 4.0-4.8, because it reads as suspicious**: Corroborated independently by PowerReviews (20M+ pages) and Northwestern Spiegel Research Center, using different datasets and methodologies, landing on the same 4.0-4.7ish "sweet spot" conclusion. This cross-source agreement is unusually strong for a marketing-adjacent claim. **Solen application**: never rank-boost or badge a 5.0 salon over a 4.6-4.8 with more reviews; if a weighted sort ships, use a Bayesian average that rewards volume. **Smallest lever**: don't sort pure-descending by star average. **Confidence: medium-high.**

7. **A single, real study (Harvard/Luca, Yelp) shows star rating causally moves revenue for independent businesses**: Regression-discontinuity design on Seattle restaurants (Washington State tax data, 2003-2009): a 1-star increase produced 5-9% revenue increase, concentrated in independents with no other reputation signal (near-zero for chains). This is the strongest causal (not merely correlational) evidence in the entire dataset for the ratings-to-revenue link. **Solen application**: since nearly all Solen salons are independents, the rating display carries outsized real weight; invest in frictionless post-booking review prompts. **Smallest lever**: reduce the taps needed to leave a review after a completed booking. **Confidence: high.**

8. **Defaults are one of the most robust findings in behavioral science and transfer directly to booking flows**: A meta-analysis of 58 datasets/73,675 participants (Jachimowicz et al., Behavioural Public Policy) found defaults shift choice by d=0.68, a 27-point absolute swing, strongest in purchase contexts. This is independently reinforced by Madrian & Shea's 401(k) natural experiment (49% to 80%+ participation) and Johnson & Goldstein's organ-donation experiment (42% to 82%). Three separate rigorous studies, same direction, large effect. **Solen application**: pre-check the most sensible option at every decision point in booking (any available staff, saved card, an eligible credit). **Smallest lever**: pre-select instead of leaving unselected states. **Confidence: high.**

9. **Progress indicators and progressive disclosure reduce abandonment and cognitive load in multi-step flows**: This is a well-established interaction-design principle (Nielsen's progressive disclosure, formalized 1995) plus GoodUI's aggregation of 10+ real A/B tests (12,961-43,113 visitors each) on progress bars, though exact percentage lifts are proprietary/paywalled so no single number should be quoted. **Solen application**: add a lightweight step indicator ("2 of 4") to the booking flow; keep secondary filters behind a "Filters" sheet rather than inline. **Smallest lever**: a dot/segment progress bar at the top of booking. **Confidence: medium.**

10. **Page speed has a real, large, production-measured effect on conversion, specifically at the product-to-add-to-cart step**: Google/fifty-five/Deloitte's "Milliseconds Make Millions" (37 sites, 30M+ sessions): a 0.1s speed improvement produced +8.4% conversion and the effect was largest between Product Detail Page and Add-to-Basket (+9.1%). This is real production telemetry, not a lab study, and directly names the funnel edge most analogous to Solen's PDP-to-service-selection step. **Solen application**: budget PDP-to-service-selection speed as its own metric, above general homepage speed. **Smallest lever**: prioritize this specific transition in perf work. **Confidence: high.**

11. **Professional photography causally increases marketplace demand, independent of underlying quality**: Peer-reviewed (Management Science) difference-in-differences study on 7,423 Airbnb listings over 16 months found professional photos produced 8.98% higher occupancy versus otherwise-similar host-photographed listings. This is one of the few genuinely causal (not correlational) studies in the whole set. **Solen application**: set a minimum photo-quality bar at salon onboarding; consider ranking/badging incentives for professional photo sets. **Smallest lever**: a soft photo-quality gate in onboarding. **Confidence: high.**

12. **Zero-results dead ends lose most searchers, but a well-designed recovery state retains a meaningful share**: Baymard's benchmark of 325+ e-commerce sites found 68% implement zero-results as a dead end with no recovery path, while a well-designed zero-results state retains an estimated 35-45% of shoppers who'd otherwise leave. This directly matches marketplace-liquidity theory (Sharetribe) and Fradkin's academic Airbnb research on stale availability, three independent sources converging on the same fix. **Solen application**: when city+category+date returns zero salons, show the nearest available date/city with one tap to relax, never a bare empty state. **Smallest lever**: build the relaxation CTA into the existing empty state component. **Confidence: high.**

13. **Real, verified availability beats broader but unreliable supply, and stale calendars are the dominant friction in two-sided marketplaces**: Fradkin's academic paper on Airbnb found 42% of booking inquiries were rejected by hosts, and each rejection cut a guest's overall platform booking probability by 43-70%; real-time availability tracking cut rejections by 59%. This is a rigorous, published academic paper with a large real dataset, one of the strongest sources in this set. **Solen application**: never render a bookable slot that isn't actually confirmed available; auto-redirect to the next real open slot on failure rather than a dead end. **Smallest lever**: eliminate stale-calendar caching in the time picker. **Confidence: high.**

14. **The aesthetic-usability effect: polish buys tolerance for friction, and first impressions form in ~50ms and predict later trust ratings**: Kurosu & Kashimura's classic ATM study (252 participants) found perceived ease-of-use correlated more with beauty than with actual usability; Lindgaard et al. (peer-reviewed, Behaviour & Information Technology) found 50ms exposure produced visual-appeal ratings highly correlated with much longer exposure. Both are established, replicated-adjacent HCI findings. **Solen application**: Solen's photography-led, generous-whitespace PDP and booking flow earn real tolerance for the multi-step flow's length; don't trade this polish for density. **Smallest lever**: protect whitespace and photo treatment even under pressure to show "more info per screen." **Confidence: high.**

15. **Accessibility failures are extremely common and concentrated in a few fixable categories that map directly onto Solen's booking/payment surfaces**: WebAIM's Million 2025 audit (1,000,000 home pages) found low contrast on 79.1% of pages, missing form labels on 48.2%, empty links/buttons on 45.4%/29.6%. This is the largest-scale, most methodologically transparent dataset in the entire research set (automated, reproducible, million-page scan). **Solen application**: audit contrast on all grey meta text, ensure every icon-only button has an aria-label, ensure every form field in booking/payment has a real label. **Smallest lever**: run an automated WCAG scan against the booking and payment components specifically, since that's the one flow a disabled user cannot route around. **Confidence: high.**

## Contested / myth-check

- **"Amazon loses 1% of sales per 100ms of latency"**: traced to an unpublished internal anecdote (Greg Linden's blog + a Stanford lecture slide), no public methodology or sample size. What IS supported: Google/fifty-five/Deloitte's real production study of 37 sites shows a genuine, measurable speed-to-conversion link, just with different (and better-sourced) numbers. Use that instead.

- **"Retention is 5-25x cheaper than acquisition"**: HBR itself qualifies this as "depending on which study you believe," with no traceable primary source for the range. What IS supported: Solen's own CAC vs. repeat-booking margin data should replace this in any internal deck.

- **"A 5% retention increase yields 25-95% more profit"**: traces to Reichheld and Sasser's single-bank branch-system finding (85% in that one case), not a cross-industry law; the "95%" figure doesn't even appear in the cited Bain brief when checked directly. What IS supported: measure Solen's own margin-per-retained-customer instead of importing this multiplier.

- **"Repeat customers spend 67% more" / "82% of companies agree retention is cheaper"**: uncredited, uncited figures repeated across marketing blogs (CXL and similar). What IS supported: nothing specific; pull Solen's own AOV by booking-number cohort.

- **"Multi-step forms complete 86% higher" / "3x completion rate" vs single-page**: no named primary study behind these specific multipliers (voiceforms/toksick blog sources). What IS supported: Baymard's actual finding that checkout length and field count correlate with abandonment; the direction is plausible but the multiplier is fabricated marketing copy.

- **"Progress indicators reduce abandonment 20-25%"**: this specific percentage traces to secondary blogs with no named primary study. What IS supported: GoodUI's real (but paywalled/no-single-number) aggregation of 10+ A/B tests showing progress bars are a good bet to test, just not a guaranteed number.

- **Deposit/prepayment cutting no-shows 40-90%**: every source is a booking-software vendor blog (Fresha, Kitomba, GlossGenius, Simple Salon, Dingg) with no peer-reviewed backing; one blog's "NIH research" citation could not be traced to a real NIH publication. What IS supported: the sunk-cost mechanism is theoretically sound and one real study (Germany, PMC) shows online/self-service booking with reminders cutting no-shows 69% relatively, but that conflates booking-channel with prepayment, not prepayment alone.

- **App vs. mobile-web LTV multipliers ("25% vs 15% retention," "45% higher LTV," "157% higher conversion")**: these recur nearly verbatim across three app-install SDK vendors (Mobiloud, Tapcart, Cordial), each with a commercial incentive to sell app adoption, with zero disclosed methodology. What IS supported: Comscore's actual retail data shows mobile browser converts a larger purchase share than apps despite less time-spent share; don't gate first bookings behind an app install.

- **Amazon "35% of revenue from recommendations"**: an independent University of Florida study (Anuj Kumar, ~2 million page views) found the real net lift is closer to 11% once cannibalization of the item already being viewed is subtracted (recommended items +9%, focal item -1.9%). What IS supported: budget any recommendation carousel at roughly 10% incremental lift, not 30-40%.

- **Social proof (download/star counts) reliably drives adoption**: a 2026 GitHub-downloads study (arxiv 2603.07919) found star/download counts had negligible measured effect, a direct contradiction of the general assumption. What IS supported: don't add a raw popularity ticker without A/B testing it on Solen's own traffic first.

- **"Only X spots left" / scarcity messaging always converts**: real effect exists only when tied to true, verifiable scarcity (CXL's Bob & Lush case, +27.1% over 6 weeks, and a 22,000-CTA-variant analysis showing real-time scarcity beats generic urgency by 19%); but other academic work (Tandfonline 2023) found scarcity cues also raise measurable frustration/stress. What IS supported: only ever show a real, live count; never a fabricated or vague one, consistent with Solen's existing no-fabrication rule.

- **Choice overload ("more options always reduce conversion") via the classic jam study**: the original Iyengar & Lepper jam-display experiment (6 jams converted ~10x better than 24) is real but a 2010 meta-analysis of 50 studies (Scheibehenne et al.) found essentially no overall choice-overload effect on replication; it needs specific conditions (high complexity, no dominant option, time pressure) to appear. What IS supported: treat curated top-N service lists as a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed win.

- **Airbnb Instant Book removing accept/decline improves outcomes**: only vendor/blog-level sourcing (beesetups.com), not an independently published controlled experiment. What IS supported: Solen's booking model is already instant-confirm; there's no strong evidence for changing it, but also no strong evidence to cite if defending it externally.

- **McKinsey retention/personalization stats ("15-25% revenue lift," "71% expect personalization")**: these are self-reported survey and cross-sectional benchmarking data (McKinsey couldn't even be directly verified in one case, "Next in Loyalty" fetch failed during research), correlational not causal, and growth could cause investment in personalization rather than the reverse. What IS supported: treat as directional motivation only, not a modeling input.

- **Brand-consistency "23-33% revenue increase"**: from a self-reported, cross-sectional survey commissioned by a design-tool vendor (Lucidpress/Demand Metric), not a controlled experiment. What IS supported: consistency is good practice, but don't cite this number as causal proof.

- **Bundling/decoy-pricing effects (Ariely subscription study, Chernev bundling research)**: real lab experiments but with small samples in artificial choice settings, not live commerce A/B tests. What IS supported: the direction (a 3-tier ladder shifts share toward the middle option; bundling a premium item with a cheap one can anchor the premium down) is worth testing on Solen's own data, not assuming will replicate at the same magnitude.

- **Post-purchase upsells convert "3-5x" better than pre-checkout**: industry-aggregated vendor blog figures (checkoutchamp.com) with no controlled study behind them. What IS supported: the directional logic (ask after commitment) is sound and consistent with the referral-timing literature below, but don't quote a multiplier.

- **Referral-prompt timing multipliers ("16x more likely to share," "2200% conversion increase")**: untraceable to any primary study, appear across Extole/ReferralCandy/Talon.One blogs. What IS supported: post-purchase/peak-satisfaction is the right moment to ask (consistent direction across multiple weak sources), just not those numbers.

- **Two-sided referral rewards, referral CAC benchmarks, reward-sizing heuristics**: all vendor/industry-blog sourced (GrowSurf, Voucherify, SICUS, Mangomint) with no disclosed methodology. What IS supported: the one rigorous source in this whole cluster is the Wharton/Journal of Marketing bank study (peer-reviewed, ~10,000 customers, 3 years) showing referred customers have 16% higher value and 18% higher retention, but that's banking, not beauty, so magnitude may not transfer.
