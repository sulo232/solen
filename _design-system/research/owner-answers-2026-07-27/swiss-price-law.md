## SHORT ANSWER

Under Swiss law, for the services Solen lists, **"ab CHF 65" is not permitted on the offer itself.** SECO's sector-specific information sheet for hairdressers, barbershops, cosmetic institutes and body care (dated 01.04.2025) says so in the exact words, in all three national languages:

> "Die angegebenen Preise sind **fixe Preise**. Angaben wie «Bart schneiden: **ab Fr. 30.-**» oder «Epilation: ab Fr. 20.- bis Fr. 50.-» **sind nicht zulässig**."
> FR: "Les prix indiqués seront des prix fixes. Les mentions telles que « taille barbe **dès 35 fr.** » … **ne sont pas admises**."
> IT: "I prezzi indicati, quindi, devono essere importi fissi. Indicazioni quali «taglio barba **da 30 fr.**» … **non sono ammesse**."

The legal way to express variable pricing is **differentiation and classification into fixed-price tiers**, or a **stated rate** where the price genuinely depends on time.

## 1. What the PBV requires for services

**PBV = SR 942.211**, current consolidated version **Stand 1. Januar 2025**. I diffed the 2022-07-01 and 2025-01-01 texts: the only substantive change is Art. 16 (comparison prices, AS 2024 621). **Art. 10, 11, 13, 14, 20, 21 are unchanged.**

**Art. 10 Abs. 1 PBV** (verbatim):
> "Werden den Konsumentinnen und Konsumenten in den folgenden Bereichen Dienstleistungen angeboten, so muss **mit dem Angebot stets der tatsächlich zu bezahlende Preis in Schweizerfranken** bekanntgegeben werden:
> a. Coiffeurgewerbe; … d. Kosmetische Institute und Körperpflege; e. Fitnessinstitute, Schwimmbäder, Eisbahnen und andere Sportanlagen; …"

Solen's whole catalogue is inside this list: hairdressers and barbers = **lit. a**, nail salons and body care = **lit. d** (SECO's sheet names "Nagel- und Fusspflege" explicitly), spas/wellness = **lit. d**, with **lit. e** catching fitness/pool-type facilities.

**Three load-bearing points:**

**(a) "mit dem Angebot stets"** — the words "mit dem Angebot stets" were *inserted* by the amendment of 25 May 2022, in force 1 July 2022 (AS 2022 343). This was a deliberate legislative reversal of **BGer 4A_235/2020** (1 Dec 2020) and **4A_314/2021** (27 Oct 2021), where the Federal Supreme Court had held SECO's "price from the start" practice had no basis in law and disclosure shortly before contract conclusion sufficed. The Federal Council closed that gap by ordinance. **Consequence for Solen: the price must be on the offer surface, not merely at the last checkout step.** Showing the fixed price only after the customer picks a slot does not satisfy Art. 10 Abs. 1 as it now reads.

**(b) Art. 10 Abs. 2 PBV** — all-in pricing, and this one is a direct landmine for a marketplace:
> "Überwälzte öffentliche Abgaben, Urheberrechtsvergütungen sowie **weitere nicht frei wählbare Zuschläge jeglicher Art, namentlich für Reservation, Service oder Bearbeitung, müssen im Preis enthalten sein.**"

"Reservation, Service oder Bearbeitung" is literally a booking fee, a service fee, and a processing fee. **If Solen charges the consumer a non-optional booking fee, it must be inside the displayed CHF figure, not added later.** Only genuinely optional extras may be shown separately.

**(c) Art. 11 Abs. 2 PBV** — the specification rule for services:
> "Aus der Bekanntgabe muss hervorgehen, auf welche **Art und Einheit der Dienstleistung** oder auf welche **Verrechnungssätze** sich der Preis bezieht."

Plus **Art. 11 Abs. 1**: "Preisanschläge, Preislisten, Kataloge usw. müssen leicht zugänglich und gut lesbar sein."

**Art. 1 PBV** states the purpose the whole thing is read against: "dass Preise klar und miteinander vergleichbar sind und irreführende Preisangaben verhindert werden."

Statutory basis: **UWG Art. 16 Abs. 1** ("Dieselbe Pflicht besteht für die vom Bundesrat bezeichneten Dienstleistungen"), Art. 16a, 17, 18, 20.

## 2. Does the PBV permit a "from" price?

**The PBV text contains no "ab" provision at all.** The word never appears. The answer is built from Art. 10/11 (offers), Art. 13/14 (advertising) and Art. 15/18 (misleading disclosure) — and SECO's published practice, which is where the operative rule actually lives.

**There is a hard split between the OFFER and pure ADVERTISING.**

### On the offer (Art. 10/11) — "ab" is not allowed

SECO coiffeur sheet, 01.04.2025, quoted above: fixed prices; "ab Fr. 30.-" not permitted.

SECO's PBV FAQ says the same thing for a specific hairdressing service:
> **Q:** "Ist eine Angabe wie «Brushing, ab CHF 43.-» gestattet?"
> **A:** "**Nein, weil die Angebote spezifiziert sein müssen.** Es ist aber möglich, die einzelnen Positionen zu unterteilen und Abstufungen vorzunehmen. Auf diese Weise ist es möglich, dem Umstand Rechnung zu tragen, dass etwa längere Haare einen grösseren Aufwand und mehr Zeit beanspruchen. Beispiel: Brushing — Kurzhaar: CHF 45.- / Mittellanghaar: CHF 50.- / Langhaar: CHF 55.-"

### In advertising (Art. 13/14) — "ab" is tolerated, but conditionally

**Art. 13 Abs. 1 PBV** is the hook, and it names the "from" case without using the word:
> "Werden in der Werbung Preise aufgeführt oder **bezifferte Hinweise auf Preisrahmen oder Preisgrenzen** gemacht, so sind die **tatsächlich zu bezahlenden Preise** bekanntzugeben."

"ab CHF 65" is precisely a *bezifferter Hinweis auf eine Preisgrenze* — a numeric lower price limit.

SECO's **Wegleitung für die Praxis 2025**, p. 17, states the condition:
> "Wenn die Werbung einen Minimalpreis angibt – z.B. «ab Fr. 59.–» – **muss genau beschrieben werden, auf welches konkrete Angebot sich der Ab-Preis bezieht.**"

And the FAQ worked example:
> **Q:** "In der Werbung eines Gartencenters werden Olivenbäume «ab CHF 59.-» angeboten. Ist das erlaubt?"
> **A:** "… Die Angabe «ab CHF 59.-» ist **die untere Preisgrenze, für welche ein Olivenbaum zu erstehen ist.** Das konkrete Angebot ist deshalb dahin gehend zu umschreiben, dass die Konsumentin oder der Konsument weiss, **was für einen Olivenbaum man für CHF 59.- erstehen kann.** Beispiel: «Olivenbäume ab CHF 59.-. Unterschiedliche Grössen, z. B. Olivenbaum, Topf Ø 24 cm, Höhe 120 cm, CHF 59.-»"

### Must the "from" price be genuinely achievable? — Yes

That FAQ answer is the direct authority: the "ab" figure **is** the lower limit *at which the thing can actually be obtained*, and the concrete offer available at exactly that price must be described. A "from CHF 65" where nothing is bookable at CHF 65 is a false price statement and engages:
- **PBV Art. 15** ("Die Bestimmungen über die irreführende Preisbekanntgabe (Art. 16–18) gelten auch für die Werbung"),
- **UWG Art. 18** ("Es ist unzulässig, in irreführender Weise: a. Preise bekannt zu geben"),
- **UWG Art. 3 Abs. 1 lit. b** (incorrect or misleading statements about "deren Preise").

One adjacent number, flagged as adjacent so you don't over-apply it: for **price-reduction ranges** (not "from" prices), the Wegleitung 2025 p. 24 permits a range only "wenn mindestens ein Beispiel für die unterste sowie auch für die oberste Preisgrenze angegeben wird und wenn die Spannbreite … nicht zu gross ist (**max. 50%**)". That 50% figure governs discount ranges, not "ab" prices. I found no equivalent numeric spread cap for "ab" prices.

## 3. When the final price genuinely can't be fixed in advance

**Important correction to a common assumption: the PBV has no "price cannot be determined in advance" escape clause.** There is no provision saying "if the final price is undeterminable, state the basis of calculation instead." I checked the full text of all 25 articles. The duty in Art. 10 Abs. 1 is unconditional.

What exists instead are **two sanctioned substitutes**, and colour-by-hair-length falls squarely in the first:

**(i) Differentiation and classification into fixed tiers** — SECO coiffeur sheet:
> "Hingegen sind eine **Differenzierung und Klassifizierung** für jede Leistung zulässig.
> Beispiel: Shampoo, Haarschnitt, Föhnen für Frauen — kurze Haare: Fr. 50.- / schulterlange Haare: Fr. 55.- / lange Haare: Fr. 60.-"

**A colour treatment priced by hair length is not an "undeterminable price" case at all.** It is exactly the case SECO says to solve with tiers. Short/medium/long, each a fixed CHF number.

**(ii) A stated rate or time-graded flat prices, where the price truly depends on time spent:**
> "Wenn der Preis von der aufgewendeten Zeit abhängt, so ist die Angabe eines **Stundenansatzes** oder von **zeitangemessenen Pauschalpreisen** zulässig.
> Beispiel: Chignon Hochzeit, zeitabhängig, Fr. 90.- / Stunde. — elektrische Epilation: 5 Min. Fr. 20.-; 10 Min. Fr. 26.-; 15 Min. Fr. 30.- usw."

This is the "basis of calculation" duty, and its home is **Art. 11 Abs. 2 PBV**'s alternative limb — "auf welche **Verrechnungssätze** sich der Preis bezieht". The Wegleitung 2025 p. 11 enumerates what counts: "Verrechnungssätze (Stundenansatz, Kilometeransatz, Taxpunkt und -wert bei zahnärztlichen Dienstleistungen, Prozent- oder Promillesatz usw.)". So: **a rate is a lawful substitute for a fixed price; a "from" price is not.**

**Materials:** SECO coiffeur sheet — the quoted price includes standard product ("Shampoo, Haarlack, Wachs, Crème"); if the customer can choose a specific more expensive product, "so muss der Preis für dieses Produkt separat angegeben werden." Relevant if Solen models premium-product upsells.

## 4. Platform vs. merchant: who carries the obligation

**Art. 20 PBV** (verbatim, unchanged since 1978):
> "Die Pflicht zur vorschriftsgemässen Bekanntgabe von Preisen und zur vorschriftsgemässen Werbung im Sinne dieser Verordnung obliegt **dem Leiter von Geschäften aller Art**."

SECO's FAQ repeats this verbatim as its answer to "Wer ist verantwortlich, dass die Preise korrekt angegeben werden?"

**Here is where I have to be straight with you: I found no SECO statement, no court decision, and no official guidance that addresses whether a booking-marketplace operator is a "Leiter eines Geschäfts" under Art. 20 PBV for prices its merchants set.** I searched SECO's FAQ, all 23 current PBV information sheets, the Wegleitung 2025, and the online-commerce sheet. The Wegleitung's "Verantwortlichkeit" section (p. 26) says only "Die Leiter von Geschäften aller Art sind verpflichtet, dafür zu sorgen, dass die Preisbekanntgabe **am Ort des Angebots und in der Werbung** vorschriftsgemäss erfolgt." That is a genuinely unsettled point, and I am not going to invent a resolution for it.

**What I can state from the text, and it points one way:**

- **SECO treats an online platform as the place of the offer.** Its online-commerce sheet defines: "Ein **Onlineshop** ist ein virtuelles Geschäft, in dem über eine **elektronische Plattform** Waren oder Dienstleistungen zum Erwerb angeboten werden." And: "Auch im Onlinehandel müssen die Konsumentin und der Konsument **von Beginn weg** über die wesentlichen Vertragsinformationen verfügen … Die Aufklärung muss **bereits beim Produkteangebot** erfolgen."

- **UWG liability does not route through Art. 20 PBV at all and reaches Solen directly.** UWG Art. 3 Abs. 1 lit. b catches whoever "über … seine Waren, Werke oder Leistungen, **deren Preise** … unrichtige oder irreführende Angaben macht **oder in entsprechender Weise Dritte im Wettbewerb begünstigt**." That last clause is written for exactly the case of publishing someone else's misleading price claim. Solen renders the price string; Solen makes the statement.

- **The enforcement machinery already names intermediaries.** UWG Art. 19 Abs. 2 lit. b puts the duty to produce information and documents on "Personen und Firmen, die Dienstleistungen anbieten, erbringen, **vermitteln** oder in Anspruch nehmen." *Vermitteln* = broker/intermediate. A cantonal PBV authority can compel Solen to hand over records.

- **UWG Art. 26** applies VStrR Art. 6/7 to "Widerhandlungen in Geschäftsbetrieben, durch Beauftragte und dergleichen."

**Practical read: contractually pushing the duty onto the salon does not clear Solen.** The salon carries its own Art. 10 duty for its own premises; that is not exclusive of Solen's exposure for what Solen's own pages display. Treat Solen as a co-carrier and design to the strict rule.

## 5. Consequences of getting it wrong

**Criminal.** PBV Art. 21: "Widerhandlungen gegen diese Verordnung werden nach den Bestimmungen des Bundesgesetzes … gegen den unlauteren Wettbewerb bestraft." → **UWG Art. 24**: intentional breach of the price-disclosure duty, of the advertising price rules, of the misleading-price prohibition, of the duty to give information, or of the Federal Council's implementing provisions → "**wird mit Busse bis zu 20 000 Franken bestraft**". Art. 24 Abs. 2: **negligence is also punishable** ("Handelt der Täter fahrlässig, so ist die Strafe Busse"). SECO's coiffeur sheet states the CHF 20,000 figure directly.

Note this is a **strict-ish, ordinance-level offence** — it does not require proof of misleading anyone. UWG Art. 23 (Freiheitsstrafe bis zu drei Jahren oder Geldstrafe) sits above it for intentional unfair competition under Art. 3, and unlike Art. 24 it is prosecuted only on complaint.

**Domain seizure.** **UWG Art. 26a** — where domain names were used for an offence under Art. 24, the prosecutor or court may order "den Widerruf des Domain-Namens zweiter Ebene, der einer Internet-Domain untergeordnet ist, für deren Verwaltung die Schweiz zuständig ist." That reaches `solen.ch`. It requires the measure to be necessary to prevent further breaches, so it is a tail risk, not a first response — but it exists and it is aimed at exactly this offence.

**Civil.** UWG Art. 9 (injunction, removal, declaratory relief, damages, disgorgement of profits under GoA rules) and **Art. 10**, which grants standing to **customers**, to **trade and industry associations**, to **consumer-protection organisations of national or regional significance**, and to **the Confederation** itself. Competing salons and competing platforms have standing. So does the Stiftung für Konsumentenschutz / FRC type of body.

**Enforcement.** PBV Art. 22: cantons supervise and refer. Art. 23: SECO holds Oberaufsicht, may issue Weisungen und Kreisschreiben to the cantons, demand information, and refer violations.

**And this sector was the 2025 campaign target.** Results published 15 December 2025: **3,651 businesses inspected** across 25 cantons in barbershops, hairdressers and cosmetic studios. 76% correct on goods, **77% correct on services**. **853 warnings** (under 25%), **8 administrative fines**, **7 criminal complaints** filed with cantonal prosecutors. SECO's stated posture is that fines and referrals follow when a business fails to fix defects despite a warning, information material and an extended deadline. The 2026 campaign moves to garages — so the acute inspection pressure on this sector has just passed, but the sheet is dated 01.04.2025 and stands.

## 6. What comparable Swiss-facing platforms actually do

I rendered these live rather than reasoning about them.

**Fresha (DE locale, Basel venue) — the compliant pattern.**
The service list shows group rows carrying "ab": `Gesicht · 10 Min. - 30 Min. · ab 19 CHF`. Tapping it opens a modal headed **"Option auswählen — Erforderlich"** with fixed prices and no way to proceed without choosing:
```
Oberlippe          10 Min.   19 CHF
Kinn               10 Min.   19 CHF
Augenbrauen        15 Min.   30 CHF
Oberlippe & Kinn   15 Min.   35 CHF
Gesicht Komplett   30 Min.   55 CHF
```
The "ab 19" is the genuine minimum, it names a real bookable option, variant choice is mandatory, and nothing bookable ever carries "ab". Their search results page shows **no prices at all**. This maps cleanly onto SECO's "Differenzierung und Klassifizierung" and onto the Wegleitung's "must describe precisely which concrete offer the ab-price refers to".

**Treatwell.ch — same shape, weaker on the listing page.**
On a venue page, bookable rows carry fixed prices (`Herren - Haarschnitt 30 Min. CHF 25`, `Herren - Bart 20 Min. CHF 20`) and "ab" appears only on a collapsed category header with its member count: `Herren - Haarschnitte & Stylings (4) ab CHF 5`. Where a category has one member it drops the "ab": `Gesichtshaarentfernung Mit Fadentechnik (1) CHF 10`. But their **city search page carries 23 instances of "ab CHF"**, including `Hair analysis 15 Min. ab CHF 0` — a "from CHF 0" is the weakest possible position under Art. 15/18 PBV and UWG Art. 3 lit. b.

**Salonkee.ch — the pattern I would not copy.**
Their search listing shows service names with no prices. But on the salon booking page, German locale, **"ab" sits on the bookable service row itself**: DOM-confirmed `<span>Ab</span> 46 CHF` for *Schneiden*, `Ab 33 CHF` for *Waschen & Föhnen*, `Ab 62 CHF`, `Ab 225 CHF` — alongside genuinely fixed rows (`Dauerwelle, Schneiden, Tönung & Föhnen 172 CHF`). That is the shape SECO's sheet names as not permitted. I could not get their variant picker to open to check whether a fixed price is forced before commit, so I cannot tell you whether it resolves downstream — I did not verify that.

**Bottom line: market practice is not a defence, and it is not uniform.** Fresha's structure is defensible; Salonkee's row-level "Ab" is the thing SECO wrote the sheet about.

## PRACTICAL RULE — encodable

**The controlling question is not "is this a from-price?" but "is this element bookable?"**

```
renderPrice(node, surface):

  # HARD BLOCK 1 — the offer surface
  if node.isBookable:                    # can be added to cart / booked directly
      require: node.price is a single fixed CHF amount
               INCLUSIVE of VAT and of every non-optional Solen fee
               (booking / service / processing — PBV Art. 10 Abs. 2)
      forbid:  "ab" / "dès" / "à partir de" / "a partire da" / "from"
      forbid:  ranges ("CHF 20–50"), "auf Anfrage", "on request", null price
      # a stated rate IS a legal alternative to a fixed amount:
      allow:   "CHF 90 / Stunde"  or  time-graded tiers ("5 Min. CHF 20; 10 Min. CHF 26")
               -> PBV Art. 11 Abs. 2 "Verrechnungssätze"

  # ALLOWED — group summary, and only under all five conditions
  elif node.isGroupHeader:
      allow "ab CHF X" IFF:
        1. node itself cannot be booked (tapping it opens a mandatory variant picker)
        2. node.variants.length >= 2
        3. every variant carries a fixed CHF price
        4. X === min(price of variants that are ACTIVE and BOOKABLE right now)
        5. X > 0
      else: render the single fixed price with no "ab"

  # ADVERTISING surface (SEO landing, banner, homepage teaser, city page card)
  elif surface.isAdvertising:
      # PBV Art. 13 Abs. 1 + Art. 14; Wegleitung 2025 p.17
      allow "ab CHF X" IFF the SAME VIEW names the concrete service
            obtainable at exactly X:
              GOOD: "Damen Haarschnitt ab CHF 65 · kurzes Haar CHF 65"
              BAD:  "ab CHF 65"
      # simpler and safer: name the service and drop the "ab"
      prefer: "Herrenhaarschnitt CHF 45"
```

**Additional invariants:**

- **Never** use "ab" as a fallback for missing or stale price data. A missing price means omit the price element, not soften it.
- **`ab CHF 0` is banned outright.** It is a lower limit at which nothing is obtainable.
- **Salon-card "ab CHF X" (cheapest service in the salon) is the riskiest pattern in the product** — it is a price limit attached to no identified service. Replace with the named cheapest service and its fixed price, or show no price.
- **The price must be visible on the offer surface, not only at checkout.** Art. 10 Abs. 1 "mit dem Angebot **stets**", as amended 1 July 2022 specifically to overturn the contrary case law.
- **Colour-by-hair-length is not a "from" case.** It is a tiering case: `kurz CHF 95 / mittellang CHF 115 / lang CHF 135`. Model it as required variants in the schema so the UI physically cannot render a "from".
- **Any non-optional Solen fee belongs inside the number.** If it is added after the displayed price, the displayed price is not the "tatsächlich zu bezahlende Preis".
- All four locales are covered identically — SECO published the ban in DE ("ab"), FR ("dès"), IT ("da"). Your EN locale should follow the same rule for "from".

**Genuinely unsettled, flagged as such:** where the boundary between "Werbung" (Art. 13, "ab" conditionally OK) and "Angebot" (Art. 10, "ab" not OK) falls on a marketplace search card that is one tap from a booking. I found no Swiss authority drawing that line. Given that a search card on Solen leads directly into a booking flow, I would treat every price-bearing surface in the product as an *Angebot* and apply the strict rule everywhere. The cost of the strict rule is a design constraint; the cost of the loose rule is CHF 20,000 per intentional breach plus standing for every competitor and consumer body in the country.
