## 1. THE ANSWER: the 7 rules that actually make a composition resolve

Each labelled with evidence tier and whether Solen already follows it.

### R1. Reuse a small closed set of numbers. The specific ratio is irrelevant.
**T1** (Wertheimer 1923, Factor of Similarity: "the tendency of like parts to band together"). This is the real mechanism under every proportion system ever sold. A modular scale, a golden ratio, a 4pt grid all work for the same reason: the same handful of values repeats, so the visual system can chunk the page. The generator does not matter. The closure does.
**Solen: FOLLOWS.** 4pt grid, three vertical tiers (32/12/16, "Three numbers, sitewide", `LOCKFILE.md:442-450`), a hand-tuned 19-role type ladder. `RATIONALE.md:126` already says it out loud: the ladder "was tuned by hand, not ratio-derived, and that is FINE... Do not retrofit a fake ratio onto it."

### R2. Grouping is a RATIO of gaps, never a pixel count.
**T1** (Kubovy & Wagemans 1995, grouping strength as a decaying exponential of *relative* distance). Space inside a group must be smaller than space around it, by a jump, not a nudge. 8 vs 12 reads as noise; 8 vs 24 reads as a boundary.
**Solen: FOLLOWS.** 32/12/16 are deliberately non-adjacent rungs, and `mt-5`/`mt-6`/`mt-7` (20/24/28px) are a *named drift signal* precisely because an adjacent rung produces the ambiguous read. This matters double for Solen because taste rule 2 bans decorative dividers, so whitespace is the *only* grouping signal, with no fallback (Material's "implicit grouping"). Honest caveat: 27 component files still contain the drift values. The law is real, the policing is INFO-level.

### R3. Alignment is a shared coordinate, and it is a number you can read.
**T1-grade as a measurement** (WCAG 3.2.3 makes the repeated-nav half normative). Edges either share an x-coordinate within ~1px of subpixel rounding, or they do not. "Poor alignment" is never a vibe; it is a delta.
**Solen: PARTIAL.** The law exists (4pt grid, container table). Nothing measures it on a rendered page. `RATIONALE.md` gap-register D3 flags this as mechanizable and not started. This is the single biggest honest gap.

### R4. Symmetry must track function, or it becomes a trap.
**T2** (Thimbleby 2002: "if the underlying system does not have the same symmetries, the visual symmetry is superficial and misleading. Users will succumb to capture errors"). Two controls may look identical only if the actions behind them are substitutable. Cancel and Delete are not.
**Solen: FOLLOWS, and this one is genuinely well built.** Twin-control rule (`LOCKFILE.md:435-437`) handles the positive case; slip-adjacency (`RATIONALE.md:217`, naming Bezahlen vs Abbrechen by name) handles the negative; `CONTROL_ELEVATION.md` supplies the mechanism (ink fill = commit, neutral outline = cancel, never two ink primaries). Live in code: `DeleteModal` and `SalonCancelModal` both ship same-size, same-position, different-weight pairs.

### R5. The box lies. Center to the ink, not to the bounding box.
**T2** (Apple HIG: "An asymmetric icon can look off center even though it's not"; Karow via Wikipedia overshoot: ~3% for O, ~5% for A; Material 3's own keyline grid encodes it as circle 20dp vs square 18dp inside the same 24dp box, an 11% overshoot).
**Solen: LAW YES, CODE NO.** Owner adopted this by name on 2026-07-15 ("circles next to squares get a small size overshoot, icon-text baselines eye-checked at 2x"), and `RATIONALE.md:144` states the same ~2-4% / ~4-8% figures. A grep for `overshoot` across the component tree returns only unrelated spring-easing comments. Round avatars sit next to square icon tiles on nearly every screen. **This is the largest open visual debt in the whole audit.**

### R6. Concentric corners: inner radius = outer radius minus the gap.
**T1, provable geometry** (two arcs share a center only at that value; Apple shipped it as an API, `ConcentricRectangle`, WWDC 2025; Material 3 states "Outer radius - padding = inner radius. For example: 48dp - 14dp = 34dp"). Get it wrong by using the same radius on both and the arcs are no longer concentric: their centers sit `gap·√2` apart along the 45-degree bisector, so **the corner gap reads about 41% wider than the flat-edge gap.** That is the exact thing the eye catches when a nested card "looks like a bug."
**Solen: LAW YES (DS-4, `LOCKFILE.md:428-433`, "the #1 'small detail' amateur tell"), CODE DRIFTS.** `CONSISTENCY_AUDIT.md:35` already catches `SalonResultCard.tsx` mixing 16/18/22 across three call sites, plus a long tail of bespoke `rounded-[10px]`...`rounded-[22px]` values across Hero, WhySolen, Footer, Header.

### R7. Content sets the module. Never pick the number first.
**T1** (WCAG 1.4.8 AAA: "Width is no more than 80 characters or glyphs"; Tinker & Paterson's 1929/1940 eye-movement work converges on 45-75; Müller-Brockmann independently landed on "7 words per line"). Three unrelated traditions hitting the same band is the strongest evidence in the entire research set.
**Solen: FOLLOWS in law, ROLLOUT INCOMPLETE.** `.prose-measure { max-width: 68ch }` shipped 2026-07-16 and is live on `SalonAbout.tsx:37`. It is **not** on `SalonReviews.tsx:190-197`, where an expanded review paragraph in the PDP's ~700px column runs an estimated 85-90+ chars per line, over both the 80ch WCAG ceiling and Solen's own tighter 68ch cap. (Estimate is container arithmetic, not a live render. Measure before claiming the fix.)

**Bonus rule, already clean:** 44px touch targets. `RATIONALE.md:153` already states the full four-way citation (WCAG 2.5.8 AA = 24, WCAG 2.5.5 AAA = 44, Apple HIG = 44pt, Material = 48dp) and correctly names Solen's 44 as the AAA/HIG comfort tier deliberately above the legal floor. Nothing to teach here.

### 8. Fix the WHY under the 4pt grid (ADD, one line, zero pixels)

Keep the rule; swap the justification to the two things that survive: decision-reduction ("the grid kills per-element debate", already the second half of that same line) and rem-based scaling (Tailwind's scale is 0.25rem-based, so tokens honor a user's font-size preference, a real WCAG 1.4.4 property).

### 9. BOUNDARY caveat on "symmetry reads as pleasant" (ADD, doc accuracy)

Bertamini/Makin's title asks "Always, Never or Sometimes" and answers *sometimes*: the effect appears under vocal-response and affect-misattribution tasks and vanishes under key-press evaluation. Add the caveat, plus one folklore-table row banning the face-symmetry-attractiveness literature (Rhodes/Perrett) as support for a UI claim; it is a different stimulus class entangled with averageness.

## 3. VERDICT: the golden ratio and the "perfect combination"

**The owner likes this idea. The evidence does not support it. Ruling against.**

### Strongest source FOR
Fechner's 1876 experiments, which did find a preference for golden rectangles and remain the ancestor of every design blog repeating the claim. And NN/g, notably, refuses to fully debunk it: *"Some designers are fascinated with the golden ratio... Others believe that the golden ratio is no more valid than any other method used to derive sizes and proportions."* That is the best the pro-side has: a 150-year-old result and a shrug.

### Strongest source AGAINST
De Bartolo et al. 2022 (*PsyCh Journal* 11(5)), the large modern replication that ran Fechner-style 2D stimuli through modern ecological images. Verbatim: golden ratio drew a **"slight overall preference (53%, p<.001)"**, but it "often emerged as the average response and median ranking **but not as the modal, most selected, preferred ratio**," and, fatally for UI: **"found no significant preference in geometric stimuli."**

Geometric stimuli are cards, panels, rectangles. The category closest to a Solen screen is the exact category where the effect disappears.

And Markowsky 1992 (*The College Mathematics Journal* 23(1)) on the "it's everywhere in great art" half: *"much of what is presented with respect to the golden ratio in art, architecture, literature, and aesthetics is false or seriously misleading."* The Parthenon, the pyramids, the famous logos: fitted to 1.618 after the fact, not found in it.

### The arithmetic that ends it without any preference debate
Apply φ to Solen's own ladder: 16 × 1.618 = **25.89**. 24 × 1.618 = **38.83**. Neither lands on the grid. Every "precise" φ value must be rounded to ship, at which point a rational multiplier set is both more honest and easier to reason about. **φ is self-defeating as a construction method before you even reach the aesthetics.** No study required, just a calculator.

### The rest of the "perfect combination" family
- **Harmonic ratios (2:3, 3:4, 4:5): FOLKLORE for visual work.** The acoustics is real, first-rate physics: those frequency ratios are consonant through overtone alignment. The leap from *consonant sound* to *pleasing sight* is a Pythagorean analogy carried for centuries with no matching perception study. Every source consulted states the transfer as assumed. Note the coincidence trap: Solen's 3:2 photo aspect is the perfect-fifth ratio, but it is sourced to "Per Uber measurement" (`LOCKFILE.md:1177-1178`), a competitive-precedent citation. Do not let anyone retro-fit music theory onto it.
- **Le Corbusier's Modulor: FOLKLORE as a universal law.** Its reference body was revised from 1.75m to 1.83m because, per Le Corbusier, "in English detective novels, the good-looking men, such as policemen, are always six feet tall." Robin Evans documents that the female body "was only belatedly considered and rejected as a source of proportional harmony." It takes φ as an input assumption, so it inherits φ's weakness. And it answers a body-to-room question, not a screen question.
- **Van de Graaf / Tschichold canon: construction real, story false.** The 2:3:4:6 margin math checks out and Tschichold's own words confirm the ninths construction. But the "medieval scribes' secret canon" framing is a 20th-century back-projection; the source itself says they "speculate that it may be older." Irrelevant to Solen regardless: it needs a bound spine with inner and outer margins. Solen has one flat `px-4` / `md:px-6` container.
- **Modular scales: CONV, not a perceptual law.** Bringhurst's definition ("a prearranged set of harmonious proportions") is circular; it asserts harmony rather than demonstrating it. And the historical record cuts the other way: the European type-size series that survived ~400 years (6,7,8,9,10,11,12,14,16,18,21,24,30,36) was practically negotiated among printers, not derived from one ratio.

### What DOES survive, and this is the honest good news
The owner's instinct is correct. There *is* real math in a resolved composition. It is just arithmetic and geometry, not a mystical constant.

1. **√2 / ISO 216 is the control case that proves the point.** A √2:1 sheet is the unique ratio that halves into two √2:1 sheets. That is provable, independently verifiable, and the entire reason A4 exists. Lichtenberg noted it in a letter in 1786; Porstmann standardized it in DIN 476 in 1922. **Nobody in that lineage ever claimed it was beautiful.** Only that it was efficient. √2 is proof that "mathematically provable" and "visually preferred" are two different axes, and that φ has been stealing credit from the first to make claims about the second. (Zero Solen relevance today: `npm run exists "invoice pdf"` returns 0, no PDF library in `package.json`. Revisit if invoice export ships.)
2. **Concentric radius (R6) is the real "perfect combination."** `inner = outer − gap` is forced by geometry, not chosen by taste. Break it and the corner opens by a computable ~41%. This is exactly the class of thing the owner is feeling when a screen "clicks." It is math, it is provable, it is visible, and Solen already locked it and then drifted off it in code. **Lead with this when answering them.**
3. **4pt divisibility is real arithmetic:** 8 × {0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4} = 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, all whole. A 5 or 7 base does not. That is the only proportion system in the whole set with a mechanical justification instead of a beauty claim. (With the GM-07 correction from ADD #8: the payoff is decision-reduction and rem-scaling, not crispness.)
4. **Gap ratios (R2) and optical overshoot (R5)** are both quantified, both checkable, both already Solen law.

**The line to give the owner:** you are right that it is about math. The math that works is divisibility, concentric centers, gap ratios, and optical compensation. The math that does not work is a magic irrational number that fails its own replication on rectangles and has to be rounded off the grid to ship anyway.

## 5. The candidate gate: what a script can honestly check on a live page

**First, the negative finding, stated plainly because it is easy to assume otherwise:** outside touch-target size, **no mainstream tool checks off-grid spacing, axis alignment, or symmetry on a rendered page.** axe-core and Lighthouse check semantics, labels, roles, contrast. Figma plugins (Design Lint, SpacingLint) run on the design file, not the DOM, and check drift from a chosen token set, not correctness. Teams that want this write their own `getBoundingClientRect` scripts. So does Solen. `RATIONALE.md:812` already admits it: "its floors are prose, not computed... every perceptual floor is eyeballed under task focus."

### Buildable now (five boolean assertions, one Playwright script, no new dependency category)

1. **Off-grid spacing.** For adjacent siblings: `(rect2.top - rect1.bottom) % 4 !== 0` flags a non-grid gap. Horizontal equivalent for gutters. Closes gap-register D8.
2. **Broken axis.** For a sibling set meant to align: `Math.abs(rect_i.left - rect_0.left) <= 1`. The 1px tolerance is subpixel rounding; anything beyond it is a real misalignment. Closes D3.
3. **Asymmetric pairs.** Read `getComputedStyle` on both sides of a claimed-mirrored relationship (a card's left vs right padding) and assert equality. Nonzero delta = an orphan value.
4. **Nested radius (Solen's own DS-4).** `childRadius === Math.max(4, parentRadius - gap)`, computed from `getComputedStyle` + rect offsets. Fails with the measured delta: "inner radius 8px, expected 4px."
5. **Target size.** `@axe-core/playwright` with `runsOnly: ['target-size']`. Returns failing nodes with selector + bounding box. Closes D6.

Wire into the existing `e2e/visual/spine.spec.ts` route list (search-results, salon-PDP, booking-services). Same shape as axe-core's own `target-size` rule (compute geometry, compare to a rule, flag the delta), just pointed at Solen's locked contract instead of WCAG's.

### Buildable, but must never be a gate
6. **Balance centroid** (per-element bbox area × darkness × saturation, offset from the visual center). Solen's D1. Ship it as a diagnostic that prints a number ("weight sits 68% right of center") for a human to judge. **It has no valid pass/fail threshold.** See section 6.

### What a script cannot check, ever
- Whether an asymmetry was **chosen or accidental**. Only the declared intent (Grid TYPE classification) makes that decidable. Measure the result against the declaration, never against a universal "symmetric is good" default.
- Whether visual symmetry **matches functional symmetry** (R4). A linter can flag two adjacent equally-styled controls; only a human knows if one of them deletes something.
- Whether the mirror-diff **should** pass. A generic empty card passes on both axes; that is an accident of emptiness, not order.

### Rejected as a gate candidate
The crude left/right ink-coverage ratio with a 40/60 threshold. It drops the distance weighting that Solen's already-approved D1 method keeps, so it is strictly a downgrade, and the 40/60 number is invented. Adding a weaker method with a made-up threshold when a stronger one is already the documented standard is exactly what rule 15 exists to stop.

**ngo-computational-balance-model-real-math-weak-validation , Ngo-style area x distance-from-center balance/moment model, with a weak-validation caveat** [T2, domain measurement-tooling]
- Claim: Ngo et al.'s classic interface-aesthetics model computes a balance/equilibrium score as a leverage analogy: split the layout into left/right and top/bottom halves at the visual center, and for every element sum (area x distance-from-center) per half; the difference between the two half-sums is the imbalance score.
