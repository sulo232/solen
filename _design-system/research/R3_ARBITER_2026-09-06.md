# ARBITER_R3.md

Arbiter pass over the whole of round 3: the seven per-screen critique files in this folder with
their rechecks and repair rounds, the 21 look-diff runs, `diagnosis/ROOT_CAUSES.md`,
`_plans/R3_ONE_SYSTEM.md`, and all 21 fold crops plus the full-page shots under
`public/_mockups/directions-0905-r3/` (opened and looked at this pass, not read about).

Two kinds of statement below, and they are labelled every time:
**quoted** means a number lifted from a critique or look-diff file, with the file named;
**looked** means I opened the PNG and describe what is on it. Nothing here was re-rendered; the
arbiter reads and looks, it does not measure live. Where a number does not exist anywhere, the line
says so and names who should have produced it.

His complaint this round answers, verbatim: *"it looks ass. What is this design? ... ours looks like
a draft, not premium at all. Why?"* plus *"if the design system changes per screen it's gonna be
ass."*

## PART 2: THE PICK

### The recommendation

**Candidate A: no shadows anywhere, one thin grey outline around each record, and thin grey lines
between groups.**

I am overturning the pre-round recommendation, which was B. Here is why, and then the cost.

**One. A is the only system whose edge never failed on any of the seven screens.** Count the
edge-visibility findings across the whole round: A, zero. B, one (bookings rows rendered a 4%
shadow on white, which FLOORS LAW 4 names invalid by name; repaired by adding a hairline). C, two
(the same bookings rows, repaired the same way, plus profile's hero card which is still flat with no
border and no shadow today). Quoted from `bookings-list.md` and `profile.md`.

**Two. A's rule needs no judgment, and the other two do.** B's rule is "a card with a photo takes
the shadow, a card without takes the hairline". C's is "a card with a photo goes flat". Both require
someone to decide, per card, whether the photo forms the boundary, and that judgment was got wrong
twice in one round on the same element: a 48x48 corner thumbnail counted as "has a photo", so the
card went shadow-only or flat and had no visible edge. A's rule is "everything gets the same 1px
grey outline", which cannot be got wrong. Against his own sentence, "if the design system changes
per screen it's gonna be ass", a rule with no decision inside it is the safer system.

**Three. Both outside references put a thin line around a card rather than a shadow under it.**
Treatwell's search card is a 1px `#E6E8ED` border at radius 4 with box-shadow none, flat, border
only (`_design-system/references/treatwell--look-recipes.md`, Card section, measured live 2026-09-05
and never opened by any helper on this round). Airbnb's search-result card carries no shadow either
(`airbnb--look-recipe.md` row 10). Only Airbnb's rail cards get an ambient shadow. So the
premium look he is pointing at is not built on shadow.

**Four. By eye across the seven folds, A wins four screens.** Search: A and C are the two that bind
the service rows into one list; B leaves three loose boxes. Bookings: A's hairline rows scan fastest
and its next-appointment card is the only one with a boundary you can actually see. Profile: A is
the only one with both a sentence anchor naming the fact and a visible card edge. Category pills: A
is the calm grey his 2026-06-29 lock names, B is the black fill he rejected on 2026-08-16, C's white
ring reads like a radio button. C wins empty states (a real ghost preview instead of a grey block)
and payment (its ambient shadow is the only visible lift in the set). B wins confirmation, where
boxing every group genuinely does hold the screen together better than A's loose middle.

**So the evidence splits four / two / one, and I am saying so rather than pretending it is clean.**
He asked for one system, so: A.

### The cost, and it is real

**Picking A changes the one screen he already approved.** The payment step is the control. B renders
it byte for byte: bordered summary card 358x335 at y=160, shadowed payment card 358x72 at y=599,
bordered sticky bar 390x85 at y=759 (quoted, `payment-step.md`). A drops the second card and the
sticky bar's top line, replacing it with a gradient fade. C changes the radius to 20, the button to
a 12px rectangle, and the bar's line to `#EBEBEB`. Only B leaves the approved screen untouched. That
is the strongest argument against my pick and it deserves to be said in one sentence: choosing A
means editing a screen he already said yes to.

**The second cost: nothing in A ever lifts off the page.** A forbids shadow on everything, including
the sticky bar. If his "draft, not premium" reaction was partly about flatness, A does not fix it.
The diagnosis argues it was not (the approved control has exactly one shadow in its whole fold, and
confirmation with zero shadows and confirmation with five both got rejected), but that argument is
subtraction, not proof.

**And the honest caveat on my own reasoning:** A's clean edge record is partly because A was allowed
a border from the start while B and C were told to reach for shadow or flatness first. If B's card
edge rule had been applied as written on day one, B would likely show zero edge failures too. What
survives that objection is point two: B's rule still contains a per-card judgment, and A's does not.

### What flips it

**To B:** if he looks at bookings and says the six older bookings should each be in their own box
rather than separated by lines. B is the only candidate that boxes every record. It also keeps the
payment screen exactly as approved.

**To C:** if he looks at payment C beside payment A and says the shadowed card reads real and the
outlined one reads flat. Then the answer is not to thicken our own shadow by eye, it is to take
Airbnb's measured one (`rgba(0,0,0,.1) 0 8px 24px`, roughly 2.5x the alpha and 8x the blur of ours)
along with its 20px radius, which is candidate C. The cost of C is named in its own sheet: its 20px
is a third card radius on top of our 16 and 24, so adopting it retires one of ours, and three of its
rows collide with dated decisions of his.
